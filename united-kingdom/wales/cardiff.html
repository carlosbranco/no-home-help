Cardiff homeless and housing-crisis help

Immediate danger, serious illness, police, fire or ambulance: 999 or 112
Nowhere safe tonight or homeless: Cardiff Housing Options 029 2057 0750; outside office hours 029 2087 3141
Sleeping outside or concerned about someone who is: Outreach Services 029 2057 0715, 7am–7pm daily
Urgent but not life-threatening medical help: NHS Wales 111; press 2 for urgent mental-health support

Last verified: 11 November 2026

Beds, meals, opening times and eligibility can change. Call before travelling when possible. An assessment, application, referral or outreach contact does not guarantee accommodation or another service. Never pay anyone who claims they can secure a council or charity place.

Tell the worker where you are tonight, where you usually sleep, your age, whether children are with you, and whether pregnancy, disability, illness, domestic abuse, immigration restrictions or a pet affects what is safe. Ask for Welsh-language support or an interpreter if needed.

Start here: call Housing Options and say clearly that you are homeless today or at risk of losing your home. Ask to make a homelessness application. If you have nowhere tonight, call the out-of-hours number after the daytime service closes. Temporary accommodation depends on an assessment.

1. Urgent housing and street outreach

Emergency services

Phone: 999 or 112.

Call for immediate violence, serious injury, overdose, breathing difficulty, loss of consciousness, fire or another life-threatening emergency. Give the exact location or nearest landmark first. If you cannot speak on a mobile call to police, listen and press 55 when prompted.

NHS Wales emergency guidance

Cardiff Housing Options

Phone: 029 2057 0750; email: HousingOptionsCentre@cardiff.gov.uk. The published service hours are Monday, Tuesday and Friday 9am–4:30pm, and Wednesday and Thursday 10am–4:30pm. You can also ask at a Cardiff Hub.

Say that you want to make a homelessness application. Explain children, pregnancy, disability, illness, care history, domestic abuse or another reason you may be vulnerable. Outside office hours call 029 2087 3141. Emergency accommodation is assessed and not guaranteed.

Council help when homeless now

Cardiff Outreach Services

Phone: 029 2057 0715, 7am–7pm every day; email: HANR.Outreach@cardiff.gov.uk.

For people sleeping outside and for reports about a known location. The team can help with a homelessness application, accommodation routes, health, benefits, substance-use and mental-health services. Give the exact sleeping place and usual time. Contact does not guarantee that someone will be found or housed that day.

Council rough-sleeping help

StreetLink and text report

Send a StreetLink alert or text REALCHANGE to 80800 with the location and description of an adult sleeping outside. Reports go to local outreach; neither service is an emergency line or bed-booking service. Call 999 for immediate danger or a child at risk.

Send a StreetLink alert | Huggard guidance

The Wallich Rough Sleepers Intervention Team

Base: 3 Pendyris Street, Cardiff CF11 6RJ

The team works daily from 7am–3pm and checks known rough-sleeping locations. It can provide breakfast, hot drinks, sleeping bags, warm clothing and toiletries, plus housing, health, benefits and substance-use referrals.

Cardiff Rough Sleeping Services

Evening outreach and severe weather

Salvation Army outreach normally operates 5pm–9pm daily with food, hot drinks, emergency care and access routes to accommodation. When the Severe Weather Emergency Protocol is active, call Outreach on 029 2057 0715 for extra provision.

Council outreach and cold-weather information

Unsafe home or domestic abuse

Call 999 in immediate danger. RISE: 029 2046 0566, 24 hours. Live Fear Free: 0808 8010 800, free and bilingual, 24 hours.

RISE can help all genders with safety planning and practical help; refuge or another safe place depends on assessment and availability. Do not return for papers or belongings if it is unsafe.

5. Health and dental care

NHS urgent care

Emergency: 999. Urgent but not life-threatening: 111, free 24 hours. Tell the call handler if you have no fixed address, phone, transport or safe place to recover. BSL users can contact NHS 111 Wales through SignVideo.

Calling NHS 111 Wales

Cardiff and Vale Health Inclusion Service

Walk-in homeless-outreach clinics are for people who live in hostels, use day services or sleep outside. No appointment is required. Ask hostel or day-centre staff for current clinic times, or ask Cardiff Outreach on 029 2057 0715.

Clinics can include nursing, GP assessment, health checks, vaccination, sexual and reproductive health, eye care and dental referrals. Times and locations change with outreach demand.

NHS homeless outreach clinics

Register with a GP without an address or ID

Anyone can register with a GP practice in Wales. A fixed address and identification are not required. Prescriptions are free for people registered with a GP in Wales, and a free interpreter can be requested.

A practice may refuse only if its list is full or you live outside its area. Ask for an explanation if refused and call NHS Wales Shared Services on 02921 501500 for Contractor Services if you cannot find a practice.

NHS Cardiff GP registration rules

Urgent dental care

Cardiff and Vale Dental Helpline: 0300 10 20 247, 24 hours. A clinician assesses the problem and, when appropriate, books an emergency clinic. Call 999 for severe swelling affecting breathing or swallowing, uncontrollable bleeding or major facial injury.

NHS Wales dental helplines

6. Mental health, alcohol and drugs

Urgent mental-health support

Call NHS 111 and press 2 for urgent mental-health advice and support. Call 999 if life is in immediate danger. Samaritans: 116 123, free at any time.

Cardiff and Vale mental-health services

Cardiff Homeless Multidisciplinary Team

Assessment Centre: 32 Cowbridge Road East, Cardiff

For single adults accessing homelessness services. The team can provide rapid assessment and support involving mental-health workers, substance-use specialists, nursing, counselling, occupational therapy, dietetics, social work, women's specialists and employment support.

Ask Housing Options, Outreach, Huggard or The Wallich for access. This is not a public emergency department.

Council health and multidisciplinary support

Cardiff Alcohol and Drug Team

Phone: 029 2078 8300. CADT provides practical guidance, needs assessment, community-care advice and one-to-one counselling for people affected by alcohol or drug use.

Council alcohol and drug support

CAVDAS homelessness outreach and rapid prescribing

Cardiff and Vale Drug and Alcohol Service works with the council's homeless multidisciplinary team. Support includes outreach, counselling, harm reduction, take-home naloxone, blood-borne-virus and sexual-health screening, and rapid access to opioid-substitution prescribing for eligible people.

Ask an outreach or homelessness worker for access. Prescribing requires clinical assessment.

8. Benefits and emergency money

Discretionary Assistance Fund for Wales

An Emergency Assistance Payment may help an eligible person aged 16+ in extreme hardship with food, gas, electricity, clothing or emergency travel after an unexpected crisis. It is a grant, not a loan. Apply online or call 0800 859 5924.

You must live in Wales and have no other money available. Limits apply to repeat awards, and ongoing bills are not covered. A decision or payment is not guaranteed immediately.

DAF eligibility | apply

Cardiff Money Advice

Phone: 029 2087 1071. Free help with budgeting, income maximisation, benefits and debt. The team or a Hub may also assess emergency food vouchers and help with a DAF application.

Cardiff advice services

Universal Credit without a fixed address

You can claim while rough sleeping, sofa surfing or staying in a hostel. With permission, use a trusted person's, hostel, day-centre or Jobcentre address. If you lack identification, a bank account, phone, email or National Insurance number, say so: alternatives may be possible.

Tell your work coach about homelessness, mental ill health or recovery needs and ask whether the claimant commitment should change temporarily. Supported or temporary accommodation may use Housing Benefit separately.

Universal Credit guidance for homeless people

Benefits and council services at a Hub

Hubs can help with Housing Benefit, council tax, the housing waiting list, online applications and document scanning. Service days and appointment rules vary. Central Library Hub and several neighbourhood Hubs also host Money Advice and Into Work.

10. Documents, phone, mail and storage

Care-of mail at Huggard

Huggard states that people believed to use its services may receive mail care of the centre. Ask first about permission, the exact address format, collection rules and how long post is kept. The centre cannot confirm to another person whether someone uses the service.

Do not send post or leave identification, papers or belongings without explicit permission.

Huggard mail information

National Insurance number

Check old payslips, benefit letters, a tax account or UKVI account first. You may start work before receiving a number if you can prove the right to work, and do not need one to start a benefit claim.

Apply for or find a number

Prove the right to work

British and Irish citizens may use an eligible passport or a birth or adoption certificate together with an official document showing the National Insurance number. Other people may use a share code or eligible immigration documents.

Do not pay an unofficial agent. Ask Cardiff Advice Service or a regulated adviser if status is unclear.

Official right-to-work service

Replace a birth certificate or get passport advice

Order certificates through the General Register Office or relevant register office; fees apply. Contact HM Passport Office before paying if documents, referees or delivery are difficult. A charity may help with an application but cannot guarantee the fee.

Order a certificate | Passport Adviceline

Scanning, printing and storage

Central Library Hub and neighbourhood Hubs provide computers, scanning and public telephones; printing may cost. No verified open-access public luggage-storage service was found. Ask Housing Options, Huggard or The Wallich about a safe option before leaving belongings.

11. Housing law, debt and legal advice

Shelter Cymru

Urgent housing helpline: 08000 495 495, Monday–Friday 9am–4pm; email: emailadvice@sheltercymru.org.uk.

Free, independent advice about homelessness, eviction, rent or mortgage arrears, disrepair and housing rights in Wales. Shelter Cymru does not operate accommodation, kitchens or emergency beds.

Shelter Cymru contact options

Cardiff Advice Service

Citizens Advice and Speakeasy provide free, independent and confidential advice through Cardiff Hubs on benefits, debt, housing, employment, immigration, discrimination, family and consumer problems. Locations and opening times vary.

Council advice-service directory | find a Hub

Speakeasy Law Centre

Phone: 029 2045 3111; email: info@speakeasy.cymru. Office open Monday–Friday 10am–1pm; most contact is by phone or email, with some face-to-face appointments.

Free specialist advice on homelessness, eviction, disrepair, benefits, debt and employment law for Cardiff and Vale residents. The team normally aims to connect a caller with an adviser within two working days.

Speakeasy Law Centre

Housing prevention and landlord problems

Contact Housing Options before leaving a tenancy. The council can discuss rent arrears, debt, landlord relations, deposits, rent in advance and alternatives. In most cases a private renter has the right to remain until the legal process reaches a court bailiff warrant.

Council homeless-prevention help

Civil Legal Advice and possession court

Phone: 0345 345 4345, Monday–Friday 9am–8pm and Saturday 9am–12:30pm. Legal Aid may cover eligible housing, debt, discrimination and domestic-abuse problems.

If facing possession, attend the hearing and ask court staff about the free Housing Loss Prevention Advice Service. Do not miss court while waiting for another appointment.

Specialist support concerning domestic abuse, sexual violence, stalking, exploitation, trafficking, forced marriage and honour-based abuse. RISE can help with safety, securing a current home or finding another safe place. Refuge is subject to assessment and availability.

Cardiff domestic-abuse services

Domestic-abuse help for men and national support

Dyn Wales for men: 0808 801 0321; email: dyn@saferwales.com. Live Fear Free for everyone: 0808 8010 800, free, bilingual and open 24 hours.

Council safe-housing information

14. Help with pets

StreetVet

People experiencing homelessness can use the UK help form to ask about free volunteer veterinary care. Eligibility and local coverage are assessed. StreetVet is not an emergency service and does not cover existing bills or provide fostering.

Request StreetVet help

Dogs Trust Together Through Homelessness

The UK-wide scheme helps eligible dog owners with veterinary costs and finding dog-friendly services. A participating homelessness service may need to apply. Assessment applies; existing bills and every procedure are not automatically covered.

Together Through Homelessness

Tell every service about your pet

Tell Housing Options and each referrer the species, size, health, behaviour and vaccination status. Ask about pet-friendly accommodation or temporary animal care before accepting or rejecting an offer. Do not abandon an animal or arrive at a hostel expecting admission.

15. Transport and Cardiff Airport

Cardiff Bus

Customer service: 029 2066 6444, Monday–Friday 7am–6pm. Use the official site for routes, timetables, fares and live disruption. Different operators may require different tickets.

Cardiff Bus contact

Transport for Wales

Use the official journey planner for trains and participating bus routes. Ask a housing, health, council or Jobcentre worker before an essential appointment whether travel can be authorised; help is not automatic.

Transport for Wales

Travel for interviews or a new job

Cardiff Into Work may help eligible jobseekers with travel and other work costs. Ask and obtain approval before paying. A Jobcentre work coach may separately consider agreed employment costs.

Into Work support

Cardiff Airport

An integrated ticket connects a Transport for Wales train from Cardiff Central to Rhoose Cardiff International Airport station and the 905 bus to the terminal. Current total journey time is about 48 minutes. Check the day's timetable, connection and fare before travel; the airport is not a shelter.

Transport for Wales airport route

Taking an animal on Cardiff Bus

Assistance dogs travel when space is available. One well-behaved dog or small animal may travel at the driver's discretion, under control and off the seats. Ask before boarding; transport permission does not mean accommodation will accept the animal.
