Glasgow homeless and housing-crisis help

Immediate danger, serious illness, police, fire or ambulance: 999 or 112
Nowhere safe to stay, Monday–Thursday 8:45am–4:45pm; Friday to 3:55pm: Glasgow City Council 0141 287 0555
Nowhere safe outside those hours: council emergency service 0800 838 502
Sleeping outside or worried about someone on the street: Simon Community Scotland 0800 027 7466, free, 24 hours
Urgent medical or mental-health help that is not life-threatening: NHS 111; choose the mental-health option when relevant

Last verified: 11 November 2026

Beds, meals, opening times and eligibility can change. Call before travelling when possible. A homeless application, outreach contact or referral does not guarantee a particular placement. Never pay someone who claims they can secure a council or charity bed.

Tell the worker where you are tonight, where you normally sleep and whether children, pregnancy, illness, disability, domestic abuse, immigration restrictions or an animal affect what is safe. Contact the council even when you have no identification, no local connection or no phone credit.

Start here: if you have nowhere safe tonight, ask Glasgow City Council to take a homeless application and say that you need temporary accommodation. If you are outside, also call the Street Team. If the council refuses to take an application or provide a written decision, call Shelter Scotland urgently.

1. Urgent housing and street outreach

Emergency services

Phone: 999 or 112.

Call for immediate violence, serious injury, overdose, breathing difficulty, loss of consciousness, fire or another threat to life. Give the exact location or nearest landmark first.

NHS Scotland emergency guidance

Glasgow City Council: homeless application

Phone: 0141 287 0555, Monday–Thursday 8:45am–4:45pm and Friday 8:45am–3:55pm. Out of hours: 0800 838 502.

Say: “I am homeless and want to make a homeless application. I need temporary accommodation tonight.” In Scotland, the council must take an application and provide temporary accommodation when it has reason to believe you may be homeless and need it while the case is assessed.

Shelter Scotland: Glasgow council route and rights | Scottish Government homelessness guidance

In-person council homelessness teams

  • South: Rowan Park, 5 Ardlaw Street, Glasgow G51 3RR.
  • North East: 1250 Westerhouse Road, Glasgow G34 9EA.
  • North West: 30 Mansion Street, Glasgow G22 5SZ.
  • Asylum and Refugee service: 44 South Portland Street, Glasgow G5 9JJ.

Phone first when possible. Take any eviction letter, ID, benefit information or evidence of risk that you have, but missing papers should not stop you asking for help.

Addresses and application process

Simon Community Scotland Street Team

Free helpline: 0800 027 7466, 24 hours.

The Glasgow Street Team works seven days a week with people sleeping outside. Give a precise public location, time and description. Outreach can offer practical support and connect someone to services, but it cannot promise a bed.

Street Team information

Shelter Scotland housing helpline

Free phone: 0808 800 4444, Monday–Friday 9am–5pm.

Use it if the council will not accept an application, refuses temporary accommodation, offers somewhere unsafe or unsuitable, or you face eviction. Ask the council for every decision in writing. Shelter's Glasgow office is not a walk-in service.

Shelter Scotland contact options

Severe weather and immediate safety

During extreme cold or other severe weather, call the council and Street Team for the current response. Arrangements and locations can change daily. Do not wait for a weather scheme if you are homeless: make a council application at any time of year.

6. Health, dental and sexual health care

Immediate and urgent medical help

Call 999 or 112 for a life-threatening emergency. For urgent help that cannot wait but is not life-threatening, phone NHS 24 on 111. Calls are free.

When to phone 111 or 999

Register with a GP without proof of address

Anyone in Scotland can use primary care without charge. A GP should not delay or refuse registration because you cannot provide identification or proof of address. If staying under three months, ask to be treated as a temporary resident.

Ask Simon Community or Glasgow City Mission for help finding a practice and completing the form.

NHS Scotland GP registration rights

Pharmacy First Scotland

Community pharmacists can assess and treat many minor illnesses and provide NHS advice. Ask whether a consultation is free under NHS Pharmacy First Scotland and say if you have no GP.

Pharmacy First Scotland | find a pharmacy

Address: 378 Sauchiehall Street, Glasgow G2 3JZ

This is not a walk-in service. If you are not registered with a dentist, call 0141 232 6323 for daytime assessment. Outside service hours, call NHS 24 on 111. Severe swelling affecting breathing or swallowing is a 999 emergency.

Dental Hospital access information

Appointments and advice: 0141 211 8130, Monday–Friday 8:45am–4pm. Appointment-only services include STI testing and treatment, contraception, PrEP and abortion care. When closed, use NHS 24 on 111; most pharmacies can provide emergency contraception.

NHS Greater Glasgow and Clyde sexual-health guidance

8. Benefits, emergency money and housing costs

Scottish Welfare Fund: Crisis Grant

A Crisis Grant may help with immediate short-term needs after an emergency or disaster when wellbeing is at serious risk. It is administered by Glasgow City Council and does not need to be repaid. Apply before spending money when possible.

Application help: 0141 276 1177, option 1, normally Monday–Friday 10am–4pm.

Apply through Glasgow City Council | 2026 Crisis Grant rules

Scottish Welfare Fund: Community Care Grant

This can help eligible people establish or maintain a settled home, including after homelessness. It may cover essential household items, but awards depend on circumstances and budget. Apply to the council; it is not cash for a same-night hostel.

Scottish Welfare Fund overview

Universal Credit without a fixed address

You can claim while sleeping rough, sofa surfing or in a hostel. With permission, use a trusted friend, hostel, day centre or local Jobcentre as a “care of” address. Help exists if you lack ID, a bank account, internet or email.

Official homeless claimant guidance | make a claim

Citizens Advice Scotland

Free phone: 0800 028 1456 to reach a local bureau by postcode. Help to Claim Universal Credit: 0800 023 2581, Monday–Friday 8am–6pm. Advice is free and confidential.

Citizens Advice contact options

Social Security Scotland

Free phone: 0800 182 2222. Ask about Scottish Child Payment, disability, carer, funeral, heating and Job Start payments. Local advisers can arrange a community appointment and interpreters are available.

Social Security Scotland contact and local support

Housing Benefit in temporary or supported accommodation

Some people in council temporary or specified supported accommodation claim Housing Benefit for rent instead of the Universal Credit housing element. Ask the accommodation provider or council benefits team which claim applies before submitting duplicate information.

10. Documents, phone, mail and address

Use a care-of address only with permission

Universal Credit can accept a hostel, trusted person, day centre or Jobcentre as a care-of address. Ask the organisation first, confirm the exact name and postcode, how often to collect mail and how long it will be held.

Official care-of address guidance

Help with forms, email and identity checks

Simon Community Access Hub and Glasgow City Mission can help with online forms and digital access. Citizens Advice can help explain evidence requests. Never give a password, bank PIN or one-time security code to a helper.

Access Hub | Citizens Advice

Scottish birth, death, marriage or civil-partnership certificates

Order official replacement certificates from National Records of Scotland or a registrar, not an unofficial reseller. Fees apply; ask a caseworker whether a fund can help before ordering.

National Records of Scotland certificate ordering

Lost or stolen British passport

Cancel a lost or stolen passport before replacing it. Costs and identity checks apply. HM Passport Office guidance recognises homelessness and lack of a postal address as vulnerabilities, so explain the situation and ask a charity or solicitor about a supporting letter.

Replace a lost or stolen passport | passport vulnerability guidance

Protect documents and accounts

Photograph important documents if safe, record reference numbers separately and report theft to the issuer. Do not store passwords with a phone or ID. Ask a service before using its address; unapproved mail may be returned.

UK cyber-security guidance

14. Help with pets

Simon Community Access Hub veterinary sessions

The Access Hub lists visiting veterinary support. Ask reception which day the vet attends, what animals can be seen and whether registration is needed. This is not a 24-hour animal emergency service.

Access Hub weekly services

StreetVet

StreetVet offers free veterinary care to eligible pets belonging to people experiencing homelessness in supported locations. Complete the help form; coverage, registration and treatment depend on assessment. It does not provide general pet fostering or pay existing vet bills.

StreetVet help form

Tell the council and shelter about your animal

Tell every referrer the species, size, health and vaccination status before travel. Some placements cannot accept animals and some may seek a pet-friendly option or temporary help. Never abandon a pet or assume an unconfirmed hostel will admit it.

15. Transport and Glasgow Airport

Plan a public-transport journey

Traveline Scotland provides live bus, rail, Subway and ferry planning. Check the last service and fare before travelling; overnight timetables are limited.

Traveline Scotland journey planner

Glasgow Subway

Contactless, Smartcard and paper tickets are available; cash remains available at ticket offices or machines. Current fares and concessions can change. National Entitlement Card discounts on the Subway have local conditions and may not apply before 9am on weekdays.

SPT Subway tickets and current fares

Free bus travel for eligible residents

People aged 5–21 who live in Scotland can travel free on most buses with a valid Young Scot or National Entitlement Card. Eligible people aged 60+ and disabled people can use the older/disabled scheme. A card is required; travel is not free while waiting for a replacement.

Under-22 scheme | 60+ and disabled scheme

Ask about essential appointment travel

A housing, health, Jobcentre or charity worker may sometimes authorise a ticket for a specific appointment, but help is not automatic. Ask before buying a ticket, keep appointment evidence and never pay a stranger for an unofficial pass.

Ask at Simon Community Access Hub

Glasgow Airport Express 500

The official 500 bus runs between Buchanan Bus Station stance 46 and Glasgow Airport stance 1, with service through the night and frequent daytime departures. Buy on board with cash, contactless or card, or online; check live disruptions and the fare first.

If stranded at the airport, speak to airport staff or police. The terminal is not accommodation; call the council out-of-hours number if you have nowhere safe.
