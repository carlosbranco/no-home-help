Birmingham homeless and housing-crisis help

Immediate danger, serious illness, police, fire or ambulance: 999 or 112
Nowhere to stay tonight, age 25+ or age 18–24 with dependent children: council 0121 303 7410, option 3, weekdays 9am–5pm
Out of hours, age 18+: council 0121 303 2296
Age 16–25 with no dependent children: St Basils 0300 303 0099
Urgent but not life-threatening medical or mental-health help: NHS 111

Last verified: 11 November 2026

Beds, food, opening times and eligibility can change. Call before travelling when possible. An assessment, alert or referral does not guarantee accommodation. Never pay anyone who claims they can secure a council or charity place.

Tell the worker where you are tonight, where you usually sleep, your age, whether children are with you, and whether pregnancy, disability, illness, domestic abuse, immigration restrictions or a pet affects what is safe. If a service cannot help, ask for the next safe option and write down the advice.

Start here: call the council if you have nowhere tonight. If you are sleeping outside, also make a StreetLink alert so Birmingham's outreach team receives your location. For help before you lose your home, apply to the council as soon as you are at risk within 56 days.

1. Urgent housing and street outreach

Emergency services

Phone: 999 or 112.

Call for: immediate violence, serious injury, overdose, breathing difficulty, loss of consciousness, fire or another life-threatening emergency. Give the exact location or nearest landmark first. If you cannot speak on a mobile call to police, listen and press 55 when prompted.

NHS guidance on calling 999

Birmingham City Council: nowhere to stay tonight

Age 25+ or age 18–24 with dependent children: call 0121 303 7410, option 3, Monday–Friday 9am–5pm. Outside these hours, age 18+: call 0121 303 2296. Age 17 or under: call 0121 675 4806.

The council will assess your circumstances. Say if you cannot safely remain where you are, and if children, pregnancy, abuse, disability or serious health needs are involved. Temporary accommodation is not guaranteed.

Council emergency-accommodation instructions

Council homelessness prevention: at risk within 56 days

Apply online: use the council form if you have somewhere tonight but may become homeless within the next 8 weeks. Submit notices, tenancy papers and income information if available, but do not wait until the final day to ask for help.

Documents: the council may ask for identity, immigration, benefits, income, bank and housing records. Call even if some documents are missing and explain why.

Apply for homelessness help

StreetLink: alert the rough-sleeping outreach team

Use it for: yourself or someone sleeping outside. Give a precise public location, usual time and description. In Birmingham the alert goes to the Trident Reach outreach team.

StreetLink is not an emergency service and does not itself provide a bed. Use 999 for immediate danger and the council numbers above if you have nowhere tonight.

Make a StreetLink alert or self-referral

Severe Weather Emergency Protocol

Birmingham activates extra emergency provision during severe weather warnings and when the forecast real-feel temperature is 0°C or below. Trident Reach coordinates access.

Access: make a StreetLink self-referral or attend SIFA Fireside during its opening hours. Ask whether SWEP is active that day. A referral still requires contact and assessment.

Council rough-sleeping and SWEP information

Domestic abuse and an unsafe home

All survivors at risk of homelessness: call the Cranstoun and council housing service on 0121 633 1750, Monday–Friday 9am–5pm. Women-only homelessness hub: 0121 272 8905, Monday–Thursday 9am–4:30pm.

Do not return to collect documents if doing so is unsafe. Refuge addresses are confidential and a place depends on assessment and availability.

5. Health and dental care

NHS urgent care

Emergency: 999. Urgent but not life-threatening: 111 or NHS 111 online. Tell the adviser if you have no fixed address, phone, transport or safe place to recover.

NHS 111 online

Homeless Health Exchange

Address: Attwood Green Medical Centre, 30 Bath Row, Birmingham B15 1LZ

Phone: 0121 301 3900. Open Monday–Friday 9am–12:30pm and 1pm–5pm; closed weekends and bank holidays.

Specialist NHS primary care for people who are homeless, unstably housed, refugees or seeking asylum. Services include GP registration, physical and mental health care, wounds, vaccinations, substance-use support and blood-borne-virus testing.

NHS Homeless Primary Care Team

Register with a GP without ID or a fixed address

NHS rules say you do not need ID, proof of address, immigration status or an NHS number. A surgery may use a temporary address or its own address. A practice can refuse for another permitted reason, such as a closed list, but must explain a refusal in writing.

NHS GP registration guidance

Dental Service for Homeless Adults

Address: Third-floor dental clinic, Attwood Green Health Centre, 30 Bath Row, Birmingham B15 1LZ

Phone: 0121 466 6880. Walk-in Thursday 1:30pm–4pm for people aged 16+ who are homeless; first come, first served until full.

NHS charges can apply unless you qualify for free treatment. The clinic may occasionally close, so call before travelling. For urgent dental pain outside the clinic, use NHS 111.

NHS homeless dental service

SIFA's current client timetable includes a nurse, vision care, chiropody and sexual-health visits on selected weekdays. These sessions can change each month; ask reception during the morning drop-in or check the current newsletter.

8. Benefits and emergency money

Universal Credit without a fixed address

You can claim while sleeping rough, sofa surfing or staying in a hostel if otherwise eligible. With permission, use a trusted person's, hostel's, day centre's or Jobcentre's address. Tell Jobcentre staff if you lack ID, internet, email, a bank account or a National Insurance number.

GOV.UK Universal Credit for homeless people

Housing Benefit for supported or temporary accommodation

People in many hostels, refuges, supported housing or council temporary accommodation must claim Housing Benefit rather than Universal Credit housing costs. Ask the accommodation worker which claim is correct.

Council Benefits Service: 0121 464 7000.

Birmingham Housing Benefit claims

Crisis and Resilience Fund

For Birmingham residents in hardship who need help with food, energy, water or essential items. Applications are assessed, funds are limited and the scheme is not a same-day emergency service.

Contact: 0121 634 7100; crf@bvsc.org.

Crisis and Resilience Fund 2026–27

Discretionary Housing Payment

If Housing Benefit or Universal Credit housing costs do not cover your rent, ask the council about a Discretionary Housing Payment. It is means-tested, temporary and not guaranteed.

Apply for discretionary housing help

Council cost-of-living support

Phone: 0121 303 1116, Monday–Friday 9am–5pm for immediate money, food, fuel or benefit problems. Ask what emergency or local support is open that day.

Birmingham living-cost support

Benefits check and casework at SIFA, Crisis or St Basils

These services can help start or repair a claim, collect evidence, contact a work coach and explain housing costs. St Basils is the dedicated route for ages 16–25; SIFA and Crisis have their own assessment rules.

10. Documents, phone, mail and storage

Council assessment documents

Bring what you safely have: photo ID, immigration papers, children's birth certificates, benefit or income evidence, bank statements, tenancy agreement and any notice requiring you to leave. Do not delay an urgent call because a document is missing.

Council list of documents

National Insurance number

You may be able to find the number on a payslip, P60, tax account or HMRC app. You can start a Universal Credit claim without knowing it. Apply for a number if you have the right to work in the UK and do not already have one.

Apply for a National Insurance number

Right-to-work evidence

If you hold digital immigration status, GOV.UK can create a share code for an employer. Get regulated immigration advice if your status or work conditions are unclear.

Prove your right to work

Passport advice if homeless or missing documents

Contact the Passport Adviceline before paying for an application. Ask a SIFA, Crisis, St Basils or migrant-support caseworker to help explain your circumstances and arrange a safe delivery address. Never use a service's address without written permission.

HM Passport Office Adviceline

Phone, SIM card and online access

Birmingham Central Foodbank may have phones or SIM cards; SIFA provides internet and digital support; the Library of Birmingham has free Wi-Fi and computers. Availability and eligibility vary.

Foodbank additional support

Mail and storage

Ask your named worker whether the council, hostel, SIFA, Crisis, St Basils or Jobcentre can authorise a care-of address. Ask where identity documents can be stored safely. No verified open-access luggage-storage service was found in Birmingham.

Do not: send post or leave belongings at any service without permission. Ask about collection deadlines and what happens if you move.

Official care-of address options for Universal Credit

14. Help with pets

StreetVet Birmingham

StreetVet provides free veterinary outreach for pets belonging to people experiencing homelessness, usually through partner services and at least two Birmingham outreach clinics each month. Locations and dates change; use the official help form or ask SIFA or an outreach worker.

StreetVet is not a general emergency vet. In an animal emergency, contact the last vet, PDSA, RSPCA or any veterinary practice for immediate advice.

StreetVet Birmingham

Dogs Trust Together Through Homelessness

The scheme can help participating homelessness services with free veterinary treatment and maintains a directory of dog-friendly accommodation. Ask your council, SIFA, Crisis or hostel worker to check the current referral route.

Dogs Trust homelessness support

Tell every service about your pet

Many placements cannot accept animals; some can. Tell the council, StreetLink and any referrer the species, size, vaccination status and whether temporary foster care is possible. Do not abandon an animal or arrive at an unknown hostel expecting it to be admitted.

15. Transport and Birmingham Airport

Plan and pay for local travel

Bus, tram and train fares vary. Use Transport for West Midlands to compare tickets and concessionary passes. If a journey is for housing, health or a Jobcentre appointment, ask the referring worker before travelling whether any ticket support is available.

Transport for West Midlands journey planner

Older and disabled travel passes

Eligible West Midlands residents can receive free off-peak bus, train and tram travel. Proof and residence rules apply; a support worker or Disability Resource Centre can help with the application.

Travel discounts and free passes

Workwise when starting a job

Eligible job starters may receive two free four-week passes and 50% off a third. Apply close to the start date and ask a Jobcentre work coach for application help if you lack internet or documents.

Workwise rules and application information

Birmingham Airport

Trains run between Birmingham New Street and Birmingham International. The Air-Rail Link connects the railway station and airport terminal; it is currently operating a reduced single-train service, with a signed walking alternative. Allow extra time and check before travel.

If you are stranded at the airport, speak to airport staff or police. The airport is not an accommodation service; use the council emergency route if you have nowhere to stay.
