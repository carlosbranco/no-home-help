Liverpool homeless and housing-crisis help

Immediate danger, serious illness, police, fire or ambulance: 999 or 112
Sleeping outside or nowhere safe tonight: Always Help Available / No Second Night Out 0300 123 2041, 24 hours
Council homelessness and emergency housing: 0151 233 3044, day or night
Urgent but not life-threatening medical or mental-health help: NHS 111; select the mental-health option for urgent mental-health support

Last verified: 11 November 2026

Beds, meals, opening times and eligibility can change. Call before travelling when possible. An assessment, outreach contact or referral does not guarantee accommodation. Never pay anyone who claims they can secure a council or charity place.

Tell the worker where you are tonight, where you usually sleep, your age, whether children are with you, and whether pregnancy, disability, illness, domestic abuse, immigration restrictions or a pet affects what is safe. If a service cannot help, ask for the next safe option and write down the advice.

Start here: if you have nowhere safe tonight, call 0300 123 2041. Also contact Liverpool City Council and say clearly that you want to make a homeless application. If you may lose your home within eight weeks, ask for prevention help now.

1. Urgent housing and street outreach

Emergency services

Phone: 999 or 112.

Call for immediate violence, serious injury, overdose, breathing difficulty, loss of consciousness, fire or another life-threatening emergency. Give the exact location or nearest landmark first. If you cannot speak on a mobile call to police, listen and press 55 when prompted.

NHS guidance on calling 999

Always Help Available / No Second Night Out

Phone: 0300 123 2041, 24 hours. Call if you are sleeping outside or have nowhere to stay tonight. The Whitechapel Centre operates this service for the council.

Give your location, circumstances and a safe way to make contact. The team can arrange an assessment and connect you with outreach or the temporary Night Assessment Hub when appropriate. The hub has a confidential address and is accessed through the service: it is not a walk-in shelter and a bed is not guaranteed.

Whitechapel urgent help | council rough-sleeper help

Liverpool City Council homeless application

Phone: 0151 233 3044, day or night. Use the council's online application as well when you can.

Say clearly that you are homeless or at risk and want to make an application. Explain children, pregnancy, disability, illness, care history, domestic abuse or another reason you may be especially vulnerable. Emergency accommodation depends on the council's legal assessment.

Council homelessness help and application

Whitechapel City Region Outreach

Phone: 0300 123 2041. Outreach normally operates Monday–Friday from 7am and at other times when necessary.

Give an exact public sleeping location, time and description. Workers can assess needs and involve health or mental-health nurses. Contact does not guarantee that a person will be found or offered accommodation that day.

City Region Outreach

Severe weather

Liverpool makes extra provision during severe weather, including when night temperatures are forecast at 2°C or below and during some storms or high winds. Call 0300 123 2041 and ask whether arrangements are active. Access still requires contact and assessment.

Council severe-weather information

Unsafe home or domestic abuse

Call 999 in immediate danger. For housing help call 0151 233 3044. Do not return for papers or belongings if it is unsafe.

National Domestic Abuse Helpline: 0808 2000 247, 24 hours. Men's Advice Line: 0808 801 0327. LGBT+ helpline: 0800 999 5428.

Council housing help if you need to flee

8. Benefits and emergency money

Liverpool Crisis and Resilience Fund

Phone: 0800 456 1523 or 0151 233 3053, Monday–Friday 8am–6pm.

Liverpool residents aged 16+ on a low income may receive short-term help after a sudden financial crisis. Possible help includes food, utilities, clothing, hygiene, furniture, travel, phone or internet and some rent shortfalls or housing costs. You do not have to receive benefits.

An award is not guaranteed. Urgent cases may be decided within 48 hours. Apply online, by phone, through a One Stop Shop or support organisation.

Fund rules and application

Universal Credit without a fixed address

You can claim while rough sleeping, sofa surfing or staying in a hostel. With permission, use a trusted person's, hostel, day-centre or Jobcentre address. If you lack ID, a bank account, phone, email or National Insurance number, say so: alternatives may be possible.

Tell your work coach about homelessness, mental ill health or recovery needs and ask whether your claimant commitment should change temporarily.

Universal Credit guidance

Citizens Advice Liverpool

General advice: 0808 278 7840, Monday–Friday 9am–5pm. Free advice on benefits, housing, work, family, immigration, consumer problems and discrimination.

Debt: 0300 330 1196, Monday–Friday 9am–5pm. High-demand drop-ins may close when full.

Citizens Advice | debt help

Council benefits advice and appointments

The council can check entitlement, help with forms and challenges, and refer urgent problems. For a face-to-face or phone benefits appointment call 0151 233 3009, Monday–Friday 8am–6pm.

Appointments use selected branch libraries. Say if you have no internet, phone, ID or safe correspondence address.

Benefits advice | One Stop Shops

Housing Benefit in supported accommodation

Housing Benefit can still apply to some hostel, supported, sheltered or council-arranged temporary accommodation even when other costs use Universal Credit. Ask the provider and council which claim is correct.

10. Documents, phone, mail and storage

National Insurance number

Check old payslips, benefit letters, a tax account or UKVI account first. You may start work before receiving a number if you can prove the right to work, and do not need one to start a benefit claim.

Apply for or find a number

Prove the right to work

British and Irish citizens may use an eligible passport or a birth or adoption certificate together with an official document showing their National Insurance number. Other people may use a share code or eligible immigration documents.

Do not pay an unofficial agent. Ask Citizens Advice or a regulated adviser if status is unclear.

Official right-to-work service

Replace a birth certificate

Order through the General Register Office or relevant register office. Fees apply. Ask a support worker about safe delivery and possible fee help.

Order a certificate

Passport advice

Contact HM Passport Office before paying if documents, referees or delivery are difficult. A charity may help you apply but cannot guarantee the fee.

Passport Adviceline

Phone and online access

Libraries provide free Wi-Fi, and Central Library has walk-up computers for members. The Crisis and Resilience Fund may assess phone or internet costs; equipment or credit is not guaranteed.

Library internet | crisis fund

Mail and document storage

Ask whether a hostel, day centre, trusted person or Jobcentre can authorise a care-of address. Confirm collection rules.

Do not send post or leave identification, papers or belongings at a service without explicit permission. No verified open-access public luggage-storage service was found.

14. Help with pets

StreetVet Liverpool

Volunteer vets provide a monthly city-centre outreach clinic for pets belonging to people experiencing homelessness. Care can include checks, vaccinations, microchipping, parasite treatment, medication and pet essentials.

The public date and location are not fixed on the official page. Use the help form or ask Whitechapel. StreetVet is not an emergency service.

StreetVet Liverpool | request help

Dogs Trust Together Through Homelessness

The UK-wide scheme helps eligible dog owners with veterinary costs and finding dog-friendly services. A participating homelessness service may need to apply. Assessment applies; existing bills and every procedure are not automatically covered.

Together Through Homelessness

Tell every service about your pet

Some placements accept pets and others do not. Tell the council and referrer the species, size, health, behaviour and vaccination status. Ask for a pet-friendly option before accepting or rejecting a placement.

Do not abandon an animal or arrive at an unknown hostel expecting admission.

15. Transport and Liverpool John Lennon Airport

Merseytravel journey planning

Use the official planner and live updates for buses, trains and ferries. Ask a housing, health, council or Jobcentre worker before an appointment whether travel can be authorised; help is not automatic.

Timetables and planner | live disruptions

Liverpool ONE Travel Centre: 1 Canning Place, L1 8LB, Monday–Saturday 10am–5pm. Queen Square: Queen Square Bus Station, L1 1RG, Monday–Saturday 8am–6:30pm and Sunday 10am–5pm.

Ask about routes, fares and tickets. Holiday hours can differ.

Merseytravel Centres

Emergency travel costs

The Crisis and Resilience Fund may help an eligible resident with necessary bus fares during a sudden financial crisis. Apply before buying a ticket when possible.

Crisis and Resilience Fund

Liverpool John Lennon Airport by bus

The 500 links Liverpool ONE and the airport about every 30 minutes; Arriva publishes a journey of about 30 minutes and service from around 4am to midnight. Routes 80A and 86A also connect the city, Liverpool South Parkway and airport.

Check the day's timetable and fare. The terminal is not accommodation; if stranded with nowhere safe, call 0300 123 2041.

Current airport bus routes

Airport connection by train

Liverpool South Parkway is the nearest main rail station. Continue by an 80A, 86A or other current airport bus. Some rail tickets can include the bus connection when the destination is Liverpool John Lennon Airport.
