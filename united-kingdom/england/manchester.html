Manchester homeless and housing-crisis help

Immediate danger, serious illness, police, fire or ambulance: 999 or 112
Nowhere safe to stay tonight: Manchester City Council 0161 234 5001, 24 hours for genuine emergencies
Housing assessment and advice, weekdays 9am–4:30pm: 0161 234 4692
Sleeping outside, weekdays 9am–4:30pm: council outreach 0161 234 5339; out of hours 0161 234 5001
Urgent but not life-threatening medical or mental-health help: NHS 111; select option 2 for urgent mental-health support

Last verified: 11 November 2026

Beds, meals, opening times and eligibility can change. Call before travelling when possible. An assessment, alert or referral does not guarantee accommodation. Never pay anyone who claims they can secure a council or charity place.

Tell the worker where you are tonight, where you usually sleep, your age, whether children are with you, and whether pregnancy, disability, illness, domestic abuse, immigration restrictions or a pet affects what is safe. If a service cannot help, ask for the next safe option and write down the advice.

Start here: call the council if you have nowhere safe tonight. If you are sleeping outside, also contact the outreach team or make a StreetLink alert with your exact location. If you may lose your home within 56 days, ask the council for homelessness prevention help now.

1. Urgent housing and street outreach

Emergency services

Phone: 999 or 112.

Call for immediate violence, serious injury, overdose, breathing difficulty, loss of consciousness, fire or another life-threatening emergency. Give the exact location or nearest landmark first. If you cannot speak on a mobile call to police, listen and press 55 when prompted.

NHS guidance on calling 999

Manchester City Council: nowhere safe tonight

Emergency-only phone: 0161 234 5001, 24 hours. Use it when there is an immediate threat to health or safety and you have nowhere to stay at night.

Daytime housing assessment and advice: 0161 234 4692, Monday–Friday 9am–4:30pm. Say clearly that you want to make a homeless application and whether you need emergency accommodation that day.

The council must assess the circumstances; temporary accommodation depends on the law and the assessment. Explain pregnancy, children, disability, illness, violence, care history or another reason you may be especially vulnerable.

Council emergency contacts

Council prevention help: at risk within 56 days

Ask for help as soon as you may lose your home. Take an eviction notice, tenancy papers, messages from the person asking you to leave and identity or income records if you have them, but contact the council even when documents are missing.

An online referral is not the route for a same-day emergency. Call the numbers above when you cannot stay safely tonight.

Council homelessness prevention and advice

Manchester rough-sleeping outreach team

Phone: 0161 234 5339, Monday–Friday 9am–4:30pm. Outside these hours: 0161 234 5001.

Give a precise sleeping location, usual time and identifying description. The team works within Manchester city boundaries and cannot guarantee that it will find someone or provide a bed on the first contact.

Report someone sleeping rough

StreetLink

Make an alert or self-referral for a person sleeping outside. Include the exact public location and when the person is usually there. StreetLink is not an emergency service and does not itself provide accommodation.

Make a StreetLink alert or self-referral

A Bed Every Night and severe-weather provision

Manchester participates in A Bed Every Night and activates extra severe-weather arrangements. Access is normally through the council, outreach or a partner assessment, not by arriving at a building without referral.

Ask whether provision is active and whether a referral is appropriate. A referral is not a promise of a bed, and the location may change.

8. Benefits and emergency money

Manchester Cost of Living Advice Line

Freephone: 0800 023 2692, Monday–Friday 9am–4:30pm.

Ask about benefits and rent, debt and bills, food, internet, laptops, phones or data. Translation and SignVideo access are available. The line gives advice and referrals; it cannot guarantee a grant.

Cost of Living Advice Line

Universal Credit without a fixed address

You can claim Universal Credit when homeless. With permission, use a trusted person's address, a hostel or day-centre address, or the Jobcentre address. DWP should offer help if you lack ID, internet, email, a bank account or a National Insurance number.

Ask Barnabus, Booth Centre, Cornerstone, Mustard Tree or Citizens Advice before using any organisation's address.

Official Universal Credit guidance for homeless people

Manchester Crisis and Resilience Fund

Manchester residents on a low income who face a sudden crisis and cannot afford essentials may be able to get short-term help with food, energy, household items or travel to a new job. Each application is assessed and help is not guaranteed.

Crisis and Resilience Fund options

Council cash grant

Eligible Manchester residents aged 16+ with no savings and certain means-tested benefits can apply for a £30 or £60 emergency grant. The crisis must be outside their control, and a previous grant within 12 months usually prevents another award.

Approval is not automatic and the scheme does not replace broken furniture or white goods.

Cash-grant eligibility and application

Housing Payments for a rent shortfall

From April 2026, Manchester Housing Payments replaced Discretionary Housing Payments. You may apply if you receive Housing Benefit or the Universal Credit housing-cost element and live in Manchester, or the council placed you outside Manchester.

Payments are not guaranteed. For a paper form or help call 0161 234 5678, Monday–Friday 9am–4:30pm.

Manchester Housing Payments

Housing Benefit in supported accommodation

Housing Benefit can still apply in some supported, sheltered or temporary accommodation. Ask the provider and council which claim is correct.

GOV.UK Housing Benefit guidance

10. Documents, phone, mail and storage

National Insurance number

Check old payslips, benefit letters or a tax account first. You may start work before receiving a number if you can prove the right to work.

Apply for a National Insurance number

Prove the right to work

Some people use a share code; others use original documents. Do not pay an unofficial agent. Ask Citizens Advice or GMIAU if your status is unclear.

Official right-to-work service

Replace a birth certificate

Order through the General Register Office or relevant local register office. Fees apply. Ask a support worker about safe delivery or fee help.

Order a certificate

Passport advice

Contact HM Passport Office before paying if documents, referees or a delivery address are difficult. Charities cannot guarantee fees.

Passport Adviceline

12. Migrants, asylum and exploitation

Office: 1 Delaunays Road, Crumpsall, Manchester M8 4QS

Free immigration and asylum legal advice is requested through an online referral, normally open Monday–Thursday. The office is appointment-only.

Boaz Trust Restricted Eligibility Support Service

For non-UK nationals aged 18+ in Greater Manchester who are single or a couple, have restricted eligibility or no recourse to public funds, and are homeless or at risk. Boaz and Booth Centre provide outreach with GMIAU immigration advice.

The online referral is normally reviewed weekly. It is not a same-day accommodation guarantee and is not designed for families.
