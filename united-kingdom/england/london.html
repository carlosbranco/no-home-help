London homeless and housing-crisis help

Immediate danger, serious illness, police, fire or ambulance: 999 or 112
Sleeping rough tonight: StreetLink 0300 500 0914
Nowhere safe to stay or losing your home: Shelter 0808 800 4444
Urgent but not life-threatening medical help: NHS 111

Last verified: 11 November 2026

Beds, opening times and eligibility can change. Call before travelling when possible. An outreach alert, assessment or referral does not guarantee a bed. Never pay someone who claims they can secure a council or charity place.

London services are often organised by borough. Tell the worker where you are sleeping, which borough you last lived in, whether you have children, pregnancy, disability, health needs or a pet, and whether violence makes your current place unsafe. Ask what to do tonight if no place is immediately available.

Start here: contact the housing or homelessness team at your London borough as soon as you are homeless or may lose your home within 8 weeks. If you are already sleeping outside, make a StreetLink alert as well. StreetLink sends the details to an outreach team; it is not an emergency line.

1. Urgent help

Emergency services

Phone: 999 or 112.

Call for: immediate violence, serious injury, overdose, trouble breathing, loss of consciousness, fire or another life-threatening emergency. Give the exact location or nearest landmark first.

NHS guidance on calling 999

StreetLink — rough-sleeping outreach

Phone: 0300 500 0914.

Use it for: yourself or someone sleeping rough. Give a precise public location, the time the person is usually there and a description. The alert goes to the local outreach team, which will try to find and assess the person. StreetLink does not itself provide accommodation.

Make a StreetLink alert

Your London borough homelessness team

When to contact: if you are homeless now or likely to become homeless within 8 weeks. In England, councils must assess eligible applicants and may provide prevention help, emergency housing or longer-term support depending on the circumstances.

Out of hours: search the borough website for its emergency homelessness number. Say clearly if children, pregnancy, domestic abuse, disability or another vulnerability is involved.

Find the correct council and apply for help

Shelter emergency housing helpline

Phone: 0808 800 4444, Monday to Friday 8am–5pm; closed bank holidays.

Use it for: urgent housing advice if you are homeless, have nowhere to sleep, face abuse or harm at home, or may lose your home. Shelter gives advice but does not allocate beds.

Shelter London services and current hours

Severe Weather Emergency Provision

What it is: short-term emergency accommodation during severe weather. London guidance says winter provision is activated when temperatures are forecast to reach 0°C or below; boroughs arrange local access and may also respond to extreme heat.

Access: make a StreetLink alert and contact the borough's rough-sleeping or out-of-hours team. Ask whether SWEP is active that day. Provision is temporary and availability can change.

4. Health and dental care

NHS urgent care

Emergency: 999. Urgent but not life-threatening: 111 or NHS 111 online.

Use 111 for: urgent medical or mental-health help, advice about where to be seen and out-of-hours care. Tell the adviser if you have no fixed address, phone, transport or safe place to recover.

NHS 111 online

Registering with a GP without ID or an address

Right to register: NHS guidance says a GP practice must not refuse you solely because you are homeless or lack proof of address, identification, immigration status or an NHS number. A practice can still refuse for another permitted reason, such as a closed patient list.

If refused: ask for the reason in writing and take it to a day centre, Healthwatch or another GP practice.

NHS leaflet for people without proof of address

5. Benefits, transport and public facilities

Universal Credit without a fixed address

Eligibility: people sleeping rough, sofa surfing or staying in a hostel can claim if they otherwise qualify. A trusted friend's, hostel's, day centre's or Jobcentre's address may be used with permission.

If you lack ID, internet, email, bank account or a National Insurance number: tell the Jobcentre. Official guidance describes alternative identity checks, assisted claims and payment options.

GOV.UK — Universal Credit if homeless

Housing Benefit for hostel or supported accommodation

Important: Universal Credit normally covers housing costs, but people in many hostels, refuges or supported and temporary accommodation must claim Housing Benefit from the council instead. Ask the accommodation worker and borough benefits team which claim is correct.

GOV.UK — Housing Benefit

Jobcentre Plus and Help to Claim

Service: Jobcentre staff can provide computer access, help establish a care-of address, arrange identity checks and adapt work-search commitments temporarily when homelessness makes them difficult. Citizens Advice Help to Claim can support a new Universal Credit claim through the first correct payment.

Find or contact Jobcentre Plus

Jobcentre Plus Travel Discount

Benefit: eligible unemployed claimants receive 50% off many London pay-as-you-go fares and can buy child-rate season tickets. Eligibility depends on benefit, age and length of unemployment.

Apply: ask your Jobcentre work coach. The discount requires both a Jobcentre Plus photocard and an Oyster card and is not automatic.

TfL Jobcentre Plus Travel Discount

Internet, charging and toilets

Day centres: Whitechapel Mission, Manna, The Passage, The Connection, 999 Club and No. 10 publish some combination of toilets, charging, computers or internet during their opening hours.

Libraries: most London borough libraries provide public computers and Wi-Fi; access, printing, charging and membership rules vary. Ask the borough for the nearest open branch. Do not leave belongings unattended.

Find a job and local employment programmes

Start with: the official Find a Job service and a Jobcentre work coach. Ask for help creating an email address, preparing a CV, obtaining interview clothes and explaining gaps caused by homelessness.

GOV.UK Find a Job

National Insurance number

Important: you can start a Universal Credit claim without knowing your National Insurance number. Check a payslip, P60, personal tax account or HMRC app; ask Jobcentre staff for help if you cannot retrieve it.

Find a lost National Insurance number

Passport applications without a home address

Help available: HM Passport Office guidance recognises homelessness and lack of documents as vulnerability factors. A third party may be able to confirm your situation and accept delivery, but contact the Passport Adviceline before applying and never use an address without permission.

HM Passport Office Adviceline

Mail collection

Possible authorised addresses: Whitechapel Mission, 999 Club and Salvation Army No. 10 publish postal services for people using their centres. Universal Credit may also accept a hostel, trusted person, day centre or local Jobcentre as a care-of address.

Ask first: do not put any service's address on an application until staff have agreed and explained collection rules.

Whitechapel Mission postal-address service

Referral line: 07932 830 440; email: info@streetstorage.org.

Service: free, secure storage in London for people experiencing homelessness or unsafe housing, including clothes, tents, sleeping bags, work tools and documents. Self-referrals are accepted. Call for the correct location, opening time and item limits before travelling.

Shelter London legal service

Phone: 0345 345 4345, Monday–Friday 9am–8pm and Saturday 9am–12:30pm.

Service: free and confidential legal-aid advice for eligible housing, domestic-abuse, debt and discrimination matters. Ask for a callback if you cannot afford the call; interpreting is available.

Civil Legal Advice eligibility and contact

Law Centres

Service: independent free legal help in some London boroughs on housing, benefits, employment, immigration and community-care matters. Each centre has its own catchment, appointments and capacity.

Find a Law Centre

Praxis immigration advice

Phone advice: 020 7749 7605, normally Wednesday 2pm–5pm except when a drop-in clinic is held.

Who: people in London on very low income or experiencing destitution. Praxis prioritises human-rights applications and people facing homelessness; it cannot assist with every immigration category. Monthly drop-ins have very limited first-come appointments.

Phone: 0808 8010 503, free, 24 hours a day.

Who: people who have claimed or intend to claim asylum. Advisers can explain asylum support and accommodation, report destitution or homelessness, help with accommodation problems and signpost to legal representation. The service does not itself provide legal representation.

Solace — get help

Phone: 0808 800 0661, Monday–Friday 9am–5pm.

Service: housing-rights advice and connection to appropriate services for young people in England who are homeless or at risk. London accommodation usually requires referral through a local authority and is not guaranteed by a helpline call.

Centrepoint contact for help

Veterans Aid

Address: 27 Victoria Square, London SW1W 0RB

Phone: 0800 012 6867 or 020 7828 2468.

Who: former UK service personnel who are homeless, facing homelessness or in crisis. The charity can assess immediate needs including food, clothing, safe accommodation and longer-term support. Call before attending.

Veterans Aid — how help works

Op FORTITUDE — veteran housing pathway

Phone: 0800 952 0774.

Service: government-sponsored referral pathway for veterans at risk of homelessness. It can give advice and, for those at highest risk who meet the criteria, seek suitable single-person accommodation. Self-referral is accepted.

GOV.UK veteran homelessness support

StreetVet

Service: free veterinary outreach for pets belonging to people experiencing homelessness. Outreach locations and times can change; use the official contact form or ask a day-centre or Street Storage worker for the current London session.

Emergency: StreetVet is not an emergency service for animals that are not already registered. In an animal emergency, contact the last vet, PDSA, Blue Cross, RSPCA or any veterinary practice for first aid.

StreetVet contact and emergency guidance

Tell services about a pet before travelling

Why: many hostels cannot accept animals, while some severe-weather and specialist placements can. Tell StreetLink, the borough, shelter or day centre the species, size and any veterinary or behavioural needs before accepting a route or placement.
