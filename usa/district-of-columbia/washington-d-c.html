Immediate danger or medical emergency: 911
Shelter, outreach, and free transport to shelter: 202-399-7093 or 311
Mental health or substance use crisis: call or text 988, or call 888-793-4357
Crime or domestic violence support: call or text 844-443-5732

Last verified: 2026-11-11

Information can change. Call ahead when possible. A listing does not guarantee a bed, appointment, meal, service, job, benefit, or approval. Shelter is first come, first served unless a program says otherwise. If you have nowhere to sleep tonight, start with Emergency shelters.

1. Emergency shelter and outreach

If you need a place tonight, call the Shelter Hotline first. DC low-barrier shelters accept people on a first-come, first-served basis. The hotline can check current options and arrange free, accessible transport. It cannot promise that a bed will still be available when you arrive.

DC Shelter Hotline and street outreach

Phone: 202-399-7093 or 311

Hotline hours: daily, 8 a.m. to midnight; 24 hours from November 1 through March 31.

Help: shelter information, wellness checks, outreach, blankets or water when available, and free accessible transport to shelter or a warming site. Outreach normally operates Monday through Friday, 9 a.m. to 9 p.m., with longer hours during cold-weather activations.

9. Free or reduced transportation

Free transport to shelter or warming sites

Call the DC Shelter Hotline at 202-399-7093 or 311. The hotline can arrange free, accessible transport to an available shelter or extreme-weather site. Transport does not reserve a bed.

Official transport information

Metro Lift - 50% discount for SNAP recipients

People currently receiving SNAP in DC, Maryland, or Virginia can apply for 50% off Metrobus and Metrorail fares. Enrollment requires an active EBT card, a SmarTrip card, current government photo identification, and a phone number.

In-person enrollment: Transit Accessibility Center, 655 Virginia Avenue SW, Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. Walk-ins are welcome.

Official eligibility and enrollment

Senior and disability reduced fares

Metro offers 50% reduced fares for riders age 65 or older and for riders under 65 with qualifying disabilities. The disability program requires an application and Reduced Fare Photo ID.

13. Identification and vital documents

No-fee DC birth certificate and non-driver ID

Eligible DC residents experiencing homelessness may obtain a no-fee DC birth certificate or non-driver identification card through a DHS-designated access point. A provider must verify eligibility and participation in homeless services; this is not a walk-in fee waiver at every government office.

Current access points include: Downtown Day Services Center, Adams Place, 801 East, Community Connections, Zoe's Doors, Shirley's Place, Latin American Youth Center, SAMU, and Sasha Bruce.

Important: current DC rules require recent HMIS history and documented service engagements. Ask an access-point worker to check eligibility before going to Vital Records or DMV.

Official requirements and access-point list

Downtown Day Services Center document help

Address: 1313 New York Avenue NW

Official center information

18. Airport transportation

Airports are not shelters. If you are stranded without a safe place to sleep, call the Shelter Hotline before leaving a staffed public area. Metro service does not run all night.

Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport (DCA)

The airport has its own Metrorail station on the Blue and Yellow lines. Use the WMATA trip planner for current service, elevator status, and fare. Metro Lift discounts apply to eligible cards.

The Silver Line serves Dulles Airport. The station connects to the terminal through an indoor pedestrian walkway of roughly five minutes. Train hours and final departures differ by day; check before traveling late at night.

19. Housing assessment and prevention

Coordinated Assessment and Housing Placement

DC uses a decentralized, no-wrong-door coordinated-entry system. A trained provider can assess housing needs and connect eligible people to available programs. An assessment is not a housing offer and does not guarantee placement.

Where to start: a day center, shelter, street-outreach team, Miriam's Kitchen, Friendship Place, SOME, or another participating provider.

Official coordinated-entry information

Friendship Place Welcome Center

Address: 4713 Wisconsin Avenue NW, Washington, DC 20016

Phone: 202-364-1419

Housing assessment, case management, food, clothing, hygiene items, employment help, and health referrals. Hours differ by day and some services require appointments; call first.

Official drop-in and clinic information

Preventing homelessness

If you still have housing but face eviction, unpaid rent, or unsafe conditions, contact Legal Aid DC or the Washington Legal Clinic promptly. Emergency rental assistance and prevention programs often have funding limits, appointments, and documentation rules; apply before a court date or move-out when possible.

  • Washington Legal Clinic for the Homeless

20. Current directories and next steps

If you do not know where to start

  1. Call 202-399-7093 or 311 if you need shelter or street outreach.
  2. Go to the nearest open day center for toilets, hygiene, charging, case management, and multiple services in one place.
  3. Ask for a coordinated housing assessment, benefits screening, and document help.
  4. Write down the name, date, and next step after every referral. Return or call again if a program tells you to check back.
