Chicago homeless and housing-crisis help

Immediate danger, fire, police, or ambulance: 911
Shelter request and city services: 311 or 312-744-5000
Food, health, and social-service referrals: 211
Mental-health or substance-use crisis: call or text 988
Domestic violence: 877-863-6338

Last verified: November 11, 2026

Beds, waiting areas, schedules, meals, costs, and eligibility can change. Call before traveling when possible. No listing guarantees shelter, housing, treatment, benefits, money, or a job the same day. Call 911 for immediate danger or a medical emergency.

If you need shelter tonight, call 311 and write down the service-request number. Single adults age 25 and older may also go to SPARC. Families with children may go to Shield of Hope/EHARC. A shelter request is not immediate housing: capacity is limited, and waits can last days or weeks. Tell the worker about children, pregnancy, age, disability, medical needs, domestic violence, pets, and your current location.

1. Urgent help, outreach, and weather

Emergency services — 911

Phone: 911.

Call for violence in progress, serious injury, trouble breathing, unconsciousness, overdose, fire, or another life-threatening emergency. Give the exact location first.

City of Chicago guidance on 311 and 911

311 — shelter request and well-being check

Phone: 311 inside Chicago or 312-744-5000.

Ask for emergency shelter, a homeless outreach team, warming or cooling information, or a well-being check. Write down the service-request number and update 311 if your location or safety changes.

Chicago Coalition to End Homelessness — how to request help

211 Metro Chicago — 24-hour referrals

Call: 211; text: your ZIP code to 898211.

Multilingual referrals for food, shelter, health care, mental health, substance-use treatment, utility help, employment, and other services throughout Chicago and Cook County.

211 Metro Chicago

Street Medicine and outreach — The Night Ministry

Street Medicine: 773-256-7549. Health Outreach Bus and case management: 773-372-3953.

Mobile teams provide health care, food, hygiene supplies, harm reduction, case management, and connections to shelter and housing at scheduled neighborhood and CTA stops.

Current outreach, bus, library, and CTA schedule

Extreme heat and cold

Call 311 for the current warming or cooling location and hours. City warming areas are generally activated at 32°F or below; cooling measures depend on the city emergency plan.

Do not rely on an old address during severe weather. Tell 311 if a person cannot travel safely, needs a wheelchair-accessible option, or appears confused, hypothermic, or overheated.

Extreme-weather guidance

Behavioral-health crisis

Call or text: 988, 24 hours. For immediate physical danger or a medical emergency, call 911.

For an eligible nonviolent mental-health emergency without weapons, overdose, active substance use, or a medical problem, call 911 and ask whether a CARE behavioral-health response is appropriate.

2. Shelter and housing access

SPARC — 24-hour intake for single adults age 25+

Address: 2241 South Halsted Street, Chicago, IL 60608

Phone: 773-526-3707. Hours: 24 hours daily.

Walk-in shelter-placement intake with food, showers, and limited overflow space while a person waits. Capacity is about 200; when full, both waiting areas close. Call before traveling if possible.

Emergency-shelter intake guidance

Shield of Hope / EHARC — families with children

Address: 924 North Christiana Avenue, Chicago, IL 60651

Families with children may walk in to request shelter assessment and temporary accommodation while staff seek the program that fits the family. Space is limited.

Bring documents if available, but ask for help even if they are missing. Tell staff immediately about domestic violence, pregnancy, disability, medications, or a safety concern.

Chicago family shelter intake

Coordinated Entry System — housing assessment

Phone Access Point: 312-971-4178.

A housing assessment can match a person experiencing homelessness with eligible supportive or subsidized housing opportunities. It is not emergency shelter and does not guarantee housing or a short wait.

Coordinated Entry overview and access points

Pacific Garden Mission

Address: 1458 South Canal Street, Chicago, IL 60607

Phone: 312-492-9410.

Faith-based shelter, meals, clothing, and recovery support for adults. Call or arrive to ask about the current intake process and capacity. Religious services are part of the program and precede public meals.

Pacific Garden Mission — get help

Franciscan Outreach shelters

Operates low-barrier shelters with three meals, case management, showers, storage, medical and behavioral-health connections. One program serving people moving from encampments has a room for personal pets.

Access: start with 311 or SPARC; do not travel to a shelter expecting direct entry unless staff confirm placement.

Franciscan Outreach — shelter services

Lincoln Park Community Services

Interim housing with meals, showers, laundry, clothing, toiletries, referrals, and case management. The organization does not promise walk-in shelter placement.

Immediate shelter request: call 311. Ask the coordinated-entry worker or shelter-placement team whether LPCS is an available match.

Two meals, showers, laundry, clothing, hygiene supplies, mail, computers, and case management for single unaccompanied women, including trans women. Its interim shelter has limited placement; daytime entry does not guarantee a bed.

Housing and shelter linkages, food, showers, laundry, clothing, mail, storage, charging, internet, ID help, education, employment, and counseling for young adults.

Mail and safe document storage

Sarah's Circle and Covenant House publish mail service for eligible guests. Shelter and day-center case managers may also let you use an agency address for ID, benefits, or employment.

Ask how long mail is held, what identification is required to collect it, and whether the address may be used on official forms. Keep photographs of important documents in a protected account when possible.

5. Health, mental health, and recovery

Medical emergency

Phone: 911.

Call for trouble breathing, severe chest pain, unconsciousness, seizure, serious bleeding, overdose, or another life-threatening problem. Do not wait for a clinic appointment.

City of Chicago guidance on 311 and 911

Heartland Alliance Health — primary, dental, mental-health, and substance-use care

Broadway Clinic: 4009 North Broadway, Chicago, IL 60613
Englewood Clinic: 5501 South Halsted Street, Chicago, IL 60621

Appointments: 773-275-2586. Care is offered regardless of ability to pay; clinics also provide outreach in shelters, transitional housing, encampments, and streets.

Clinic hours and services

The Night Ministry — free mobile health care

Street Medicine: 773-256-7549. Health Outreach Bus: 773-372-3953.

Wound care, health assessment, chronic-disease support, food, hygiene supplies, harm reduction, case management, and referrals at street, neighborhood, and CTA locations.

Outreach and Health program

988 and Illinois CARES

Call or text: 988, 24 hours. Illinois CARES mobile-crisis line: 800-345-9049.

Free crisis support and connection to local mental-health or substance-use care. Call 911 when there is an active medical emergency, weapon, violence, or immediate threat to life.

Illinois CARES crisis information

Chicago CARE behavioral-health response

For an eligible nonviolent mental-health crisis, call 911 and ask for the CARE Team. City clinicians and emergency medical technicians can de-escalate, assess, refer, and transport to community care.

CARE is not used for weapons, violence, overdose, active substance use, a medical emergency, or an active suicide attempt. The dispatcher decides the response.

CARE eligibility and hours

Illinois Helpline — alcohol and other drugs

Call: 833-234-6343; text: HELP to 833234, 24 hours.

Confidential treatment navigation and immediate Medication Assisted Recovery support for opioid or alcohol use. In a suspected overdose, call 911 and use naloxone if available.

Illinois Helpline

Free naloxone

Cook County Health and Chicago partners distribute free naloxone, including at the CORE Center, 2020 West Harrison Street. Chicago Public Library and CDPH locations may also carry kits.

Naloxone can wear off before the opioid does. Always call 911 after giving it and follow the dispatcher's instructions.

Free naloxone locations and overdose guidance

Free STI and HIV services

Chicago Department of Public Health offers free STI/HIV testing and STI treatment at specialty clinics. Howard Brown also publishes free confidential community testing schedules.

If an HIV exposure was within the last 72 hours, ask immediately about PEP; do not wait for a routine testing day.

6. Public benefits and housing prevention

Illinois ABE — SNAP, Medicaid, and cash assistance

DHS Help Line: 800-843-6154. Apply online, by phone, or at a Family Community Resource Center.

The same application can request SNAP, Medicaid, TANF or refugee cash assistance, and Medicare Savings Programs. Ask for and save the case number.

ABE benefits application | Application help and phone numbers

SNAP help for people experiencing homelessness

Greater Chicago Food Depository benefits staff can explain SNAP rules and documents. A person without a fixed address can use a collateral statement as proof of Illinois residency.

Eligibility and work requirements changed in 2026. Ask staff to screen for exemptions rather than assuming you are ineligible.

SNAP/Link benefits help

Chicago Coalition Law Project — benefits problems

Free helpline: 800-940-1119, Monday–Friday, 9 a.m.–5 p.m.

Legal help with SNAP, TANF, Medicaid, SSI disability, benefit denials or termination, birth certificates, housing, family law, and other issues for people who are homeless or unstably housed.

Law Project

Homelessness Prevention and rental assistance

Call 311 or 312-744-5000 and ask for “rental assistance” or the Homelessness Prevention Program.

One-time aid may help with rent, a security deposit, utilities, or another documented crisis for an eligible Chicago resident who still has housing. Funding and application periods are limited.

Emergency financial assistance

Rental Assistance Program (RAP)

RAP is for Chicago residents who currently have housing but may lose it because of an eligible loss of income or emergency. It is open regardless of immigration status, subject to program rules and funds.

Call 311 or visit a DFSS Community Service Center for help in your language. It is not an emergency-shelter program.

8. Identification, legal help, and rights

Free Illinois State ID with Homeless Status Certification

An eligible applicant can receive a free standard Illinois ID by presenting a notarized Homeless Status Certification completed within 90 days by an authorized agency or professional.

The form replaces the residency document, not proof of name, date of birth, or Social Security number. Ask a shelter, day center, attorney, school homeless liaison, or other authorized provider to complete it and help gather the remaining documents.

Homeless Status Certification form

Free birth-certificate clinic — Harold Washington Library

Address: 400 South State Street, 7th floor, Chicago, IL 60605

Published schedule: Thursdays, 10 a.m.–1 p.m., with listed holiday exceptions; first come, first served and capacity-limited.

Free U.S. birth-certificate assistance for people experiencing homelessness. Be ready with your date and place of birth and parents' names. Check current clinic dates before traveling.

Law Project clinic calendar

Chicago CityKey — free municipal ID

Free optional Chicago government ID that can also work as a library card, Ventra card, and prescription-discount card. Apply online or at a scheduled printing event.

Proof of identity and Chicago residency are required; the document guide lists alternatives. Ask a service provider how to document residency if you have no fixed address.

How to apply for CityKey

Chicago Coalition Law Project

Helpline: 800-940-1119, Monday–Friday, 9 a.m.–5 p.m.

Free legal help for ages 14–24, adults, and families who are homeless or unstably housed: IDs, benefits, housing, family law, education, consumer problems, civil rights, and record sealing.

Legal clinics and help

Legal Aid Chicago

Free civil legal assistance subject to income, location, case type, and capacity. Areas include housing, public benefits, family safety, consumer, employment, and other civil matters.

Use the intake page to check current phone and online options. For criminal arrest help, ask for a public defender or call the First Defense Legal Aid hotline listed by the Coalition.

Legal Aid Chicago — get help

Rights while unsheltered

Keep your shelter-request number, photograph important papers, and ask for a written receipt if property is taken. Do not sign a form you do not understand; request language or disability accommodation.

For legal help after an encampment action, property loss, discrimination, or benefits problem, call the Coalition Law Project at 800-940-1119.

Immigrants, migrants, and refugees

Chicago states that city shelter, food, and medical-care referrals are available regardless of immigration status. Call 311 for shelter and 211 for services and language support.

For immigration legal and social-service providers, use the city resource guide. Do not pay a “notario” or unlicensed person for immigration legal advice.

If detained, do not sign documents you do not understand; ask to speak with a qualified immigration attorney.

10. Help with animals

PAWS Chicago Crisis Foster Care

Temporary foster care for up to 60 days for an eligible dog or cat when the owner faces homelessness, domestic violence, hospitalization, or another crisis. Veterinary care is provided as needed during placement.

Limits include no more than two pets, no puppies or kittens under one year, and restrictions for aggression or unmanageable medical conditions. Apply and wait for confirmation before making plans.

PAWS Crisis Foster Care

Friendship Center — pet food

Address: 2709 West Lawrence Avenue, Chicago, IL 60625

Cook County residents may request dog or cat food while shopping at the food pantry, while supplies last. There are no ZIP-code or income requirements; households may shop twice each month.

Published pantry hours from June 2026 are Monday and Thursday noon–4 p.m., Tuesday 3–7 p.m., and Friday noon–4 p.m. Confirm before traveling because food and supplies depend on donations.

Current pet-food rules and pantry hours

Pets and shelter placement

Tell 311 or SPARC the species, size, temperament, vaccination status, carrier or leash, and medical needs before accepting placement. Franciscan Outreach publishes a personal-pet room at one specialized shelter, but availability and referral rules change.

Ask about a pet-compatible placement or temporary foster care. Do not abandon an animal to enter shelter.
