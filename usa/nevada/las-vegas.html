Las Vegas — help for people without housing

Immediate danger, fire or medical emergency: 911
Local services and shelter search: 211
Courtyard Homeless Resource Center: 702-229-6117
Clark County Social Service: 702-455-4270
Mental health or suicide crisis: call or text 988
Domestic violence: call or text SafeNest at 702-646-4981

Last checked: November 12, 2026

Beds, meals, funds, hours and eligibility can change. Call before traveling when possible. No listing guarantees a bed, meal, benefit, medical appointment, treatment, bus pass, housing placement or job on the same day.

If you have nowhere safe to stay tonight, start with the Courtyard, an emergency shelter listed below, or Nevada 211. Say where you are, whether you are alone or with family, and whether there is a medical problem, violence, pregnancy, disability, veteran status, a minor or a pet. Coordinated Entry is important for longer-term housing, but it is not required before asking an emergency shelter for help.

What to do today

  1. Danger, severe illness, overdose or violence happening now: call 911 and give your exact location first.
  2. No safe place tonight: call the Courtyard at 702-229-6117, then 211 if you need another option. Ask specifically who can take your household today; a phone call does not reserve a place.
  3. Need food, a shower or social services: ask the Courtyard, Salvation Army Day Resource Center or Catholic Charities what is available today. For longer-term housing, request a Coordinated Entry assessment through Clark County Social Service at 702-455-4270.

1. Emergency help and first calls

911 — emergency response

Phone: 911.

Call for immediate danger, serious injury, breathing difficulty, chest pain, severe bleeding, overdose, fire or violence in progress. Give the address, cross streets or a clear landmark before describing what happened.

LVMPD — when to call 911

311 — police non-emergency

Local call: 311; dispatch: 702-828-3111, 24 hours.

For non-emergency police help, a past theft or vandalism, a welfare check or a report when nobody is in immediate danger. Use 911 if a crime, threat or medical emergency is happening now.

6. Benefits, rent help and housing

Access Nevada — SNAP, Medicaid and cash assistance

Use Access Nevada to screen for and apply for food assistance, health coverage and cash assistance. A library, shelter case manager or county social worker may help if you lack a device, email address or documents.

An application is not approval. Keep a confirmation number and copies of everything submitted.

Access Nevada

SNAP food benefits

Nevada states that households with little or no money should tell Social Services because they may qualify for expedited SNAP within seven business days. This is not guaranteed; an interview and eligibility review are required.

If you have no address, explain your housing situation and ask how notices and the EBT card can be received safely.

Nevada Division of Social Services — SNAP

Clark County financial and housing assistance

Phone: 702-455-4270.

County programs include financial assistance, eviction prevention, fixed-income rent assistance, utilities and some transportation support. Funding, eligibility and documents vary. Apply through the official portal or ask an office for help with a paper application.

Clark County — Applications for Assistance

Coordinated Entry for longer-term housing

Ask Clark County Social Service, the Courtyard or another listed access point for an assessment. Coordinated Entry prioritizes limited housing resources according to local criteria. It is not a waiting list with a guaranteed time or placement.

Help Hope Home — access points

The Just One Project — housing and workforce screening

Phone: 702-462-2253.

The organization screens for housing navigation, homelessness prevention, rapid rehousing, shared housing and workforce services. Submit an inquiry or call; availability depends on program criteria and funding.

The Just One Project — assistance options

Social Security and SSI without a permanent address

A person without permanent housing has the same right to apply for Social Security and Supplemental Security Income benefits as someone who is housed. Benefits may use direct deposit, Direct Express or an approved third-party arrangement.

Social Security: 1-800-772-1213. Ask a case manager or Legal Aid for help with records, appeals or safe mail.

SSA — Spotlight on Homelessness

Avoid benefit and housing scams

Do not pay for a public-benefit application, Coordinated Entry assessment or supposed priority on a shelter or housing list. Do not share bank login details, one-time codes or EBT PINs.

Ask for the worker’s name, agency, written program rules and a receipt for any legitimate payment.

8. Identification, mail and legal help

Start with a case manager

Ask the Courtyard, Clark County Social Service, Catholic Charities or another shelter case manager to list which document to replace first. Request written proof of homelessness and ask whether the program can receive mail for you before using its address.

CARE Complex and Catholic Charities publish help with IDs or Clarity Cards, but current availability and referral rules must be confirmed.

Nevada ID fee exemption for people experiencing homelessness

People under 25 who declare homelessness can receive fee exemptions for an original, renewal or duplicate Nevada ID. People 25 or older may receive a one-time exemption for a duplicate license or ID. All other identity and residency requirements still apply.

Apply in person and use the DMV 128 Declaration of Homeless Status. Ask a case manager to help gather the required original documents and a safe mailing address.

Nevada DMV — ID cards and homeless fee exemptions

Replacement Social Security card

Social Security: 1-800-772-1213. A replacement card is free, but acceptable identity and status documents are required. Some people can apply online; others need an appointment.

If documents are missing, ask Social Security and a case manager what records from shelters, schools, employers, courts or agencies can help. Do not pay a private intermediary for a replacement card.

12. Extreme heat and cold

Find an active cooling or warming station

Seasonal sites open and close according to current conditions. Call 211 or Clark County at 702-455-4270 for the location operating today. Do not rely on an old flyer or last season’s list.

Ask whether a station allows pets and whether they must be leashed or in a carrier.

Clark County CARE and heat relief

Courtyard during extreme weather

Address: 314 Foremaster Lane

The Courtyard is listed as open 24 hours and is commonly included in heat-relief planning. Call 702-229-6117 to confirm the current cooling or warming arrangement, space and pet rules.

Courtyard official information

Heat illness is a medical emergency

Confusion, collapse, seizure, fainting, very hot skin or inability to drink can indicate severe heat illness. Call 911, move to shade or air conditioning and cool the person with wet cloths while waiting. Do not force liquid into an unconscious or confused person.

Carry water, rest during the hottest hours and refill whenever a staffed site permits it.

13. Help with animals

Tell every shelter about the animal before accepting a referral

The Courtyard has pet kennels, The Shade Tree works with Noah’s Animal House for residents, and SafeNest states that it shelters survivors with children and pets. Rules and space vary. Confirm whether the animal can remain with you, what records are needed and who is responsible for care.

Do not surrender or abandon an animal based only on an unofficial promise of later reunification.

14. Practical plan and personal safety

Make one written plan

Write down: where you will sleep tonight, where you can eat, the next appointment, the worker’s name, documents still needed and a backup location. Keep the list with emergency numbers on paper as well as on your phone.

At every intake

State urgent facts clearly: child, pregnancy, disability, serious illness, medication, violence, veteran status, trafficking, recent discharge, pet or lack of transport. Ask what is available now, what is not available, the next check-in time and a specific alternative.

Ask for a written receipt, confirmation number or appointment details whenever possible.

Protect yourself from theft and exploitation

Do not hand over original documents, phone, benefit card or bank information to a stranger offering work, transport, a room or a shelter place. Meet service providers at the published address and verify their number on the official website.

Tell a trusted person where you are going. If threatened, move to a staffed place and call 911; for non-emergency police response, call 311 or 702-828-3111.
