Boston — help for people experiencing homelessness

Medical, fire, or immediate danger: 911
Shelter and city services: 311 or 617-635-4500
Adult shelter referral: 617-534-4440
Families with children or a pregnant household member: 866-584-0653

Last verified: November 12, 2026

Beds, hours, meals, addresses, and eligibility can change. Call before a long trip. No listing guarantees a bed, meal, payment, treatment, benefit, or job on the same day. Call 911 for immediate danger or a life-threatening medical problem.

If you need a safe place tonight, call the shelter before traveling or contact 311. Tell staff your age, gender identity, whether you are with children, whether anyone is pregnant, your accessibility or medical needs, and whether you have a pet. You may ask for help even if your identification is missing.

1. Emergency and street outreach

911 — immediate danger or medical emergency

Call: 911.

Use 911 for trouble breathing, severe chest pain, unconsciousness, seizure, serious bleeding, suspected overdose, fire, violence in progress, or another immediate threat. Give the exact location and a nearby landmark.

When to call 911 — City of Boston

BOS:311 — non-emergency city help

Call: 311 inside Boston or 617-635-4500.

Ask for the current shelter, warming or cooling location, city outreach, or another local service. Tell the operator if a person cannot travel safely, uses a wheelchair, has children, or has a pet.

Boston 311

Pine Street Inn Street Outreach

Outreach line published by the City: 866-633-0170.

Outreach teams engage people sleeping outside and connect them with shelter, health care, and housing. If there is immediate medical danger, call 911 instead. For a routine request, give a precise location, description, and time last seen.

Pine Street Inn Street Outreach

Massachusetts 211 — statewide resource referrals

Call: 211, available 24 hours.

Ask for a nearby meal, shelter, clothing, benefits, domestic-violence service, or other human service. Confirm the provider's hours and entry rules before traveling.

6. Benefits and longer-term housing

DTA — SNAP food benefits and cash assistance

DTA Assistance Line: 877-382-2363. Workers are available Monday–Friday, 8:15 a.m.–4:45 p.m.; automated case information is available 24 hours.

Apply for SNAP or TAFDC/EAEDC cash assistance, replace an EBT card, upload documents, or request disability and language accommodations. Press 7 to apply for SNAP. Interpreters are available.

How to contact DTA

SNAP when you do not have housing or cooking facilities

Tell DTA that you are homeless and whether you cannot store or cook food. Some households that include an older, disabled, or homeless person may qualify to buy prepared meals from participating restaurants. Eligibility and expedited service require screening.

Apply for SNAP — Massachusetts

MassHealth

Customer service: 800-841-2900; TTY: 711. Staff are available Monday–Friday, 8 a.m.–5 p.m.; interpreters are available.

Apply for or manage state health coverage and ask for an enrollment assister. BHCHP can also help patients navigate coverage.

MassHealth application help

Boston Housing Authority applications

In-person help: 52 Chauncy Street, Boston, MA 02111

Status and application help: 617-988-3400, Monday–Friday, 9 a.m.–4:30 p.m.

Public, project-based, and other waiting lists use separate online portals. BHA warns that it has no immediate housing and waits can last years; the tenant-based Section 8 list is currently closed. Ask which lists are open and whether you can document a priority.

Apply for BHA housing

Housing search and coordinated services

Ask shelter or day-center case managers for a housing assessment, help collecting homelessness verification, and referrals to supportive or subsidized housing. An assessment is not an immediate housing offer. Keep phone and mailing information current with every program.

8. Documents and legal help

Identification, birth records, and mailing address

Start with St. Francis House, Women's Lunch Place, Rosie's Place, or your shelter case manager. Ask for help obtaining a birth certificate or state ID, paying a required fee, making copies, and establishing a safe mailing address. Requirements differ by record and state.

St. Francis House document and mail help

Greater Boston Legal Services

Address: 197 Friend Street, Boston, MA 02114

Main: 617-371-1234; housing intake: 617-603-1807.

Free civil legal help for eligible low-income people, including eviction, public or subsidized housing, benefits, health, family, and shelter denials or terminations. General telephone intake is Monday–Friday, 9:30 a.m.–12:30 p.m.; housing intake is by phone.

GBLS hours and intake

Rosie's Place Legal Program — women

Legal helpline: 617-318-0271.

Legal support and advocacy for women. Call to learn intake hours and whether the matter can be handled or referred.

Rosie's Place service guide

Eviction, shelter denial, or benefits problem

Keep every notice and envelope, photograph it, and record the deadline. Call GBLS promptly. For an emergency family-shelter denial or termination, tell intake that it concerns Emergency Assistance shelter and give the date on the notice.

Before accepting a shelter placement

Tell the shelter or 311 that you have a pet, including the species, size, vaccination status, and any medical need. Ask whether the assigned site can accommodate the animal or arrange a safe alternative. Do not assume every shelter accepts pets.

11. Severe weather

Warming and cooling information

Call: 311 or 617-635-4500.

Ask for the current open warming or cooling location and its hours. Do not rely on an old address during a weather emergency. Tell 311 if a person cannot travel safely or needs an accessible option.

Boston Emergency Management

When weather becomes a medical emergency

Call 911 for confusion, fainting, severe weakness, very hot or cold skin, difficulty breathing, or inability to wake. During extreme heat, seek shade and water; during severe cold, keep clothing dry and cover exposed skin while arranging indoor help.
