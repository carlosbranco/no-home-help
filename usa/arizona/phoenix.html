Immediate danger, fire, overdose, or medical emergency: 911
Shelter and local services: 211 or 877-211-8661

Mental-health or substance-use crisis: call or text 988, or call 844-534-4673

Last verified: November 12, 2026

Beds, meals, funding, transport, hours, and eligibility can change. Call or check the linked provider page before traveling when possible. No listing guarantees a bed, meal, treatment, payment, document, benefit, ride, or job. Call 911 for immediate danger or a life-threatening medical emergency.

Phoenix uses separate access routes for single adults, families, young people, veterans, and survivors of domestic violence. Tell staff your age, household members, exact location, health or mobility needs, pregnancy, veteran status, service animal or pet, and whether you are safe tonight.

The facility is open 24 hours; Keys to Change publishes assessment and service-plan hours of 7:00 a.m.–11:00 p.m. CASS beds are assigned in person, first come and first served; they cannot be reserved by phone or email.

1. Urgent help, outreach, and extreme heat

Emergency services — 911

Phone: 911.

Call for immediate violence, fire, heat stroke, overdose, unconsciousness, severe breathing trouble, heavy bleeding, serious injury, or another life-threatening emergency. Give the exact location or nearest landmark and describe the danger first.

City of Phoenix — Office of Homeless Solutions

PHX C.A.R.E.S. — street outreach

Phone: 602-262-6251, then press 6. Published request hours: Monday–Saturday, 8:00 a.m.–5:00 p.m.

Request outreach for someone living outside or report an encampment. Give an exact location and describe urgent health or safety needs. Outreach cannot force a person to accept services and does not guarantee a shelter bed. Homelessness itself is not a crime; call 911 for immediate danger.

City of Phoenix — coordinated homeless response

Heat safety and current seasonal status

Current status: Phoenix's dedicated 2026 heat-relief sites closed for the season on October 1, 2026. Do not travel to the former 24-hour site at 20 W. Jackson Street expecting it to be open. Phoenix Public Library locations continue to offer air-conditioned space during normal hours. Call 211 for a current open location.

The regional Heat Relief Network normally operates May 1–September 30. Check the live network before relying on any seasonal site or extended hours.

City of Phoenix — 2026 heat-site closure notice | MAG Heat Relief Network

Heat illness — act early

Move to shade or air conditioning, loosen clothing, cool the skin, and sip water if the person is awake and able to swallow. Confusion, fainting, seizures, very hot skin, or inability to drink can mean heat stroke: call 911 and cool the person while help is coming.

Maricopa County — heat-relief toolkit

988 and Arizona crisis support

Call or text: 988. Arizona statewide line: 844-534-HOPE (4673), 24 hours.

Free, confidential crisis support for suicidal thoughts, emotional distress, mental-health crisis, or urgent substance-use concerns. Call 911 when there is immediate physical danger.

Solari Crisis Response Network

Arizona 211

Call: 211 or 877-211-8661.

Ask for an open shelter access point, meal, cooling location, health service, benefit office, or transport option. Give your ZIP code or nearest cross streets and confirm details directly with the provider.

Homeless ID Project — Phoenix service

Arizona identification card

A standard Arizona ID generally costs $12 and requires proof of identity, a Social Security number, and two proofs of Arizona residency. People age 65+, SSI recipients, and eligible homeless veterans may qualify for a no-fee card. A standard ID is not a federal REAL ID.

Ask Homeless ID Project or a caseworker to identify acceptable documents and residency evidence before visiting MVD.

15. Airport and onward travel

Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport

The free PHX Sky Train connects Terminals 3 and 4 with the Valley Metro Rail station at 44th Street and Washington Street. Valley Metro fare is required for the light-rail portion. Check route and operating information before relying on a late-night connection.

An airport is not a shelter. Remaining in terminals without a travel purpose may lead to contact with airport staff or police. If you need shelter, use 211 or the Welcome Center before going to the airport.

Tickets and travel help

16. A practical plan for today

  1. Handle danger first. Call 911 for immediate danger, serious illness, heat stroke, or overdose. Use 988 for a mental-health or substance-use crisis.
  2. Use the right shelter route. Single adults: Welcome Center. Families: 211 or Family Housing Hub. Ages 12–17: Safe Place. Ages 18–24: HomeBase. Veterans: VA center. Domestic violence: the confidential screening line.
  3. Protect against heat. Dedicated 2026 heat sites are closed for the season, but dangerous heat can occur outside it. Use a library during normal hours or call 211 for an open indoor location.
  4. Secure food and hygiene. Check André House, St. Vincent de Paul, NourishPHX, Key Campus, or Justa Center according to your age and today's schedule.
  5. Protect documents and medication. Keep papers dry, photograph them if safe, write down medication names, and ask about secure mail or storage. Do not use a mailing address without permission.
  6. Start benefits and ID work early. Use Health-e-Arizona Plus, Homeless ID Project, and a caseworker. Ask what alternate proof is accepted.
  7. Ask precise questions. “Is intake open now?”, “Can I enter today?”, “What ID is required?”, “Is there a fee?”, “Can you accommodate my disability, service animal, pet, medication, partner, or children?”, and “What should I do if there is no space?”
  8. Keep a second plan. Write down at least two options, the name of the person you spoke with, and the time to call back. Availability can change before you arrive.
