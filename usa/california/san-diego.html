San Diego homeless and housing-crisis help

Immediate danger, fire, overdose, or medical emergency: 911
Shelter, outreach, food, and local services: 211
City Homeless Outreach Team: 619-446-1010
Mental-health or substance-use crisis: call or text 988, or call 888-724-7240
Domestic violence: 619-234-3164 or 888-385-4657

Last verified: November 12, 2026

Beds, meals, funding, hours, eligibility, and referral routes can change. Call or check the linked provider page before traveling when possible. No listing guarantees a bed, meal, treatment, payment, document, benefit, ride, or job. Call 911 for immediate danger or a life-threatening medical emergency.

San Diego uses coordinated shelter intake. Many City-funded shelters do not accept unscheduled walk-ins. Call 211 and ask for a City of San Diego outreach specialist, or start in person at Father Joe's Villages Day Center. Tell staff your exact location, age, household members, immediate safety, medical or mobility needs, pregnancy, veteran status, whether you are living in a vehicle, and whether you have a service animal or pet.

Best first step for a single adult: call 211, or visit the San Diego Day Center at 1501 Imperial Avenue. The Day Center publishes daily hours of 7:00 a.m.–3:00 p.m. and can begin an assessment, place you on a shelter waitlist, and connect you with showers, laundry, mail, charging, meals, health care, and documents. A visit does not reserve a bed.

1. Urgent help, outreach, and bad weather

Emergency services — 911

Phone: 911. Text 911 if calling would place you in danger and text-to-911 is available.

Call for immediate violence, fire, overdose, unconsciousness, severe breathing trouble, heavy bleeding, serious injury, or another life-threatening emergency. Give the exact location or nearest landmark first.

City of San Diego — assistance directory

211 San Diego — shelter and local-service referral

Call: 211, 24 hours a day. Interpretation is available in many languages.

Ask for shelter access, a City outreach specialist, food, health care, benefits, safe parking, or another local service. Give your ZIP code or nearest cross streets and state whether you are alone, with children, under 25, a veteran, escaping violence, living in a vehicle, or have a disability. A referral does not guarantee a bed.

211 San Diego — housing and homelessness

Homeless Outreach Team

Phone: 619-446-1010.

The San Diego Police Department's Homeless Outreach Team can connect willing participants with shelter, detoxification, treatment, and other services. For non-police street outreach, call 211 and request a City outreach specialist. Outreach cannot guarantee a placement.

City of San Diego — outreach contacts

The Hub — navigation by appointment

Access: call 211 for a referral. In-person navigator meetings are by appointment.

The Hub can provide short-term navigation, housing search support, employment readiness, basic-needs connections, family reunification, and diversion from the shelter system. Calling 211 starts a referral; it does not guarantee housing or shelter.

City of San Diego — The Hub

988 and San Diego Access and Crisis Line

Call or text: 988. Local line: 888-724-7240, 24 hours, free and confidential.

For suicidal thoughts, emotional distress, or a mental-health or substance-use crisis. A clinician-led Mobile Crisis Response Team may be sent when appropriate. Call 911 for immediate physical danger or a medical emergency.

County of San Diego — get behavioral-health help

Inclement Weather Shelter Program

The City may activate additional overnight beds during cold, rain, strong wind, or other severe weather, generally from November through March. Activated beds are usually first come, first served and available for one night.

Check the live City page or call 211 on the day you need shelter. Do not travel to a weather bed unless activation and location are confirmed.

2. Shelter and housing access

City coordinated shelter intake

Start with: 211 or a City outreach worker.

The City system includes programs for adults, families, women, young people, veterans, older adults, and people with behavioral-health needs. Outreach and intake staff match people to available and appropriate beds. Most programs are referral based and cannot promise same-day placement.

People actively enrolled in CalFresh, Medi-Cal, or CalWORKs may apply for PRONTO LIFE if they are not already using another reduced-fare category. MTS publishes a $2.50 one-way fare, $6 daily cap, and $72 monthly cap for eligible regional bus and Trolley travel.

PRONTO support: 619-595-5636. A current County benefits-verification letter, valid ID, application, and release form are required. Fares and rules can change.

MTS — PRONTO LIFE

Senior, disability, and Medicare reduced fares

9. Identification and documents

California no-fee identification card

People who meet the federal definition of homelessness may qualify for a no-fee California ID card. A qualifying government agency or nonprofit must verify homelessness and issue form DL 933. The waiver applies to an ID card, not a driver license.

Ask the Day Center, Think Dignity, or another case manager whether they can issue the form and help gather identity and residency evidence before a DMV visit.

California DMV — no-fee ID program

Think Dignity ID Bank

Phone: 619-537-8736.

Help obtaining and securely storing birth certificates, Social Security cards, and California IDs. Services are offered through scheduled outreach and partner sites; check the current calendar or email listed on the official page.

Think Dignity — ID Bank

Replace a Social Security card

The Social Security Administration may allow an online replacement in some cases; others require an application and original identity documents. Replacement cards are free.

Do not pay an unofficial website. Ask a case manager or ID Bank for help if you lack identity documents, a mailing address, or online access.

Social Security Administration — replace a card

Birth certificate

For a birth registered in San Diego County within the last two years, contact the County Office of Vital Records and Statistics. For older records, the County directs applicants to the Assessor/Recorder/County Clerk. Identity and notarization rules depend on the type of certified copy requested.

If cost or identification is a barrier, ask Think Dignity or a homeless-service case manager before ordering.

San Diego County — birth certificates

Day Center mail service

Address: 1501 Imperial Avenue, San Diego, CA 92101

Ask staff to register you before using the address. Confirm the exact name and address format, accepted mail, collection hours, ID rules, and how long items are held. Do not send an ID, EBT card, bank card, or legal document until staff confirms the procedure.

Father Joe's Villages — Day Center services

Protecting documents

Photograph or scan documents and store copies in a password-protected account you can reach from another device. Keep Social Security numbers, benefit case numbers, and login codes private.

Use a City storage center for belongings and the Think Dignity ID Bank for vital-document storage when eligible. Medication cannot be stored in City storage bins.

14. Pets and service animals

Tell shelter staff about the animal before referral

State whether the animal is a trained service animal or a pet, its size and species, vaccination status, behavior around people and animals, and whether you have a carrier, leash, food, and medication. Ask for the rule in writing when possible.

Do not abandon an animal because of an unconfirmed placement. The South County Lighthouse says it may accept up to two pets, and some safe-parking or safe-sleeping options may have different rules. Confirm directly before traveling.

16. A practical plan for today

  1. If there is immediate danger or a life-threatening medical problem: call 911 and give the exact location first.
  2. If you need a safe place tonight: call 211 and ask for a City outreach specialist. State your household type, age, safety, medical needs, vehicle, veteran status, and animal.
  3. If you are a single adult downtown: go early to the San Diego Day Center, 1501 Imperial Avenue. Ask for shelter assessment, an orange badge, lunch, shower, laundry, mail, and document help.
  4. If you have children, are under 25, are a veteran, or are escaping violence: use the specialist route in this page. Do not accept a general-adult referral without stating your circumstances.
  5. Apply for benefits: call ACCESS at 866-262-9881 or visit the Metro Family Resource Center. Ask about expedited CalFresh, Medi-Cal, and General Relief.
  6. Protect your next steps: register a safe mailing address, replace ID, keep copies of every notice, and write down the name, date, and outcome of each call.
