Los Angeles homeless and housing-crisis help

Immediate danger, police, fire, or ambulance: 911
Adult or youth shelter line: 800-548-6047
Families, food, benefits, and local services: 211
County mental health and substance-use help: 800-854-7771, 24 hours

Last verified: November 10, 2026

Beds, schedules, routes, and eligibility can change. Call before traveling when possible. No listing guarantees a bed, meal, treatment, money, or job the same day. Call 911 for immediate danger or a medical emergency.

When asking for help, give your exact location or nearest landmark, whether you have a safe place tonight, when you last ate, medical needs, age, pregnancy or disability, children or pets with you, and whether you have identification. Ask the worker to explain the next step, required documents, transportation, and what to do if no bed is open.

Best first call: adults and young people seeking a bed can call 800-548-6047; households with a child under 18 should call 211. A Coordinated Entry access center can also assess housing needs. Shelter placement is based on eligibility and availability.

1. Urgent help

Emergency services — 911

Phone: 911.

Call for: immediate violence, serious injury, trouble breathing, loss of consciousness, fire, overdose, or another life-threatening emergency. Give a precise location and describe the danger first.

City of Los Angeles homeless-services guidance

LAHSA shelter line

Phone: 800-548-6047.

Use it for: adult shelter and shelter for youth ages 16–24. Tell the operator your age, household, location, health or mobility needs, pets, and whether you need transportation.

LAHSA Coordinated Entry — get help

211 LA

Phone: 211, 24 hours.

Use it for: family shelter, food, benefits, health, legal, domestic-violence, and other local referrals. Ask the operator to check current eligibility and hours rather than relying on an old address.

City crisis-response resource list

County behavioral-health help line

Phone: 800-854-7771, free and available 24 hours.

Service: mental-health and substance-use referrals, crisis assessment, field response, interpretation, and transportation when appropriate. Call 911 if there is immediate physical danger.

8. Identification and documents

California no-fee ID card

Eligibility: a person meeting the federal definition of homelessness may qualify for a no-fee California identification card.

How: ask a government agency or qualifying nonprofit serving unhoused people to verify your status and issue the DMV verification form before visiting DMV.

California DMV — identification cards

DMV verification and address letters

Documents: a qualified provider issues the no-fee verification. For a REAL ID, an applicant may use the verification plus a letter from a shelter, nonprofit, faith organization, employer, or government agency attesting California residence.

Ask first: a Coordinated Entry caseworker, mission, or public-benefits office whether it is registered to issue the form.

DMV no-fee ID program rules

ID help through a shelter or caseworker

Start with: the shelter line at 800-548-6047 or 211.

Ask for: identity-document replacement, a mailing address, fee-verification form, and help obtaining birth records. Keep copies and receipts separate from original documents.

9. Legal help

Service: free help for eligible people experiencing or nearing homelessness with housing, benefits, family law, restraining orders, tickets, license reinstatement, credit, and record clearing.
