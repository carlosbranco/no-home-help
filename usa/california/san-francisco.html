San Francisco homeless help

Immediate danger, fire, overdose or medical emergency: 911
City information and non-emergency street outreach: 311; outside San Francisco call 415-701-2311
SF Homeless Outreach Team and adult shelter line: 628-652-8000
Mental health or substance-use crisis: call or text 988
Behavioral Health Access Line: 888-246-3333, 24 hours

Last verified: November 11, 2026

Information and capacity can change without notice. Call ahead when possible. A listing does not guarantee a bed, meal, appointment, benefit, job, storage space or housing. Shelter and housing assessments are not promises of placement.

If you have nowhere safe to sleep tonight, call 628-652-8000, join the adult shelter waitlist if it fits your household, or use the walk-up and population-specific options below. Tell staff about children, pregnancy, disability, urgent health needs, violence, gender-safety needs and any animal before accepting a referral.

1. Urgent help and street outreach

Emergency services — 911

Call 911 for immediate danger, serious injury, trouble breathing, unconsciousness, fire, suspected overdose or another life-threatening emergency. Give the exact location first. If naloxone is available during a suspected opioid overdose, use it and still call 911 because its effect can wear off.

San Francisco coordinated street-response guidance

311 city services

Inside San Francisco: 311. Outside the city: 415-701-2311. TTY: 711.

Available 24 hours a day in more than 160 languages. Use 311 for non-emergency information, service referrals and a request for outreach to a person living outside. Use 911 when someone is in immediate danger.

Official 311 information

SF Homeless Outreach Team and Adult Shelter Access Line

Phone: 628-652-8000

Request street outreach, ask about adult shelter access, or register for the adult shelter reservation waitlist. An outreach request or waitlist registration does not reserve a bed. For the waitlist, use either the phone or the online form, not both.

Official 2026 shelter-access guidance

988 and Behavioral Health Access Line

Call or text 988 for suicide, mental-health or substance-use crisis support. For public behavioral-health assessment and treatment navigation, call 888-246-3333, select a language and extension 2. The access line operates 24 hours.

2. Shelter and housing entry

Know the difference: a shelter waitlist is for a temporary bed. Coordinated Entry assesses possible housing and problem-solving resources. Neither process guarantees placement.

Adult shelter reservation waitlist

Phone: 628-652-8000

For self-sufficient adults age 18 or older without minor children seeking a bed at Next Door, MSC South or Sanctuary. Register once by phone or through the official form. Keep the waitlist identifier and check the list regularly. Registration is not a same-day bed guarantee.

Official 2026 instructions and form link

Dolores Shelter Program — walk-up overnight shelter

Address: 1050 South Van Ness Avenue, San Francisco, CA 94110

Published hours: overnight, 6 p.m.–8 a.m.; the first-come line may begin at 5:30 p.m.

Walk-up option for adults without minor children. Capacity is limited and lining up does not guarantee entry. Confirm the current intake process before making a long trip.

Official shelter-access guidance

MSC South

Address: 525 Fifth Street, San Francisco, CA 94107

Phone: 415-597-7960

Adult shelter with meals, showers, case management and support. Access is through the adult shelter reservation system, 311 or SFHOT; it is not a guaranteed walk-in bed.

3. Free meals, groceries and faith-based help

GLIDE Daily Free Meals

Address: 330 Ellis Street, San Francisco, CA 94102

Meals: breakfast daily 7:30–9 a.m.; lunch 11:30 a.m.–1 p.m.; dinner 3:30–5 p.m. The first half-hour of breakfast is reserved for seniors, families and adults with disabilities.

No eligibility screening or intake is required. GLIDE is rooted in a church community; worship is not listed as a meal requirement.

Address: 121 Golden Gate Avenue, first floor, San Francisco, CA 94102

Open seven days: breakfast begins at 7 a.m.; lunch is 10 a.m.–1:30 p.m.; a weekday hot meal to go begins at 2 p.m.

Franciscan Catholic organization serving the public. Worship is not listed as a requirement.

6. Benefits, cash aid and transport

CalFresh food benefits

Phone: 855-355-5757. Apply online, by phone or at an SFHSA center. A person experiencing homelessness does not need a household address to start.

If income and money are very low, ask for Expedited Services, which may provide eligible applicants benefits within three business days. People without stable mail or phone may ask about EBT-card pickup.

Official CalFresh application instructions

County Adult Assistance Programs — CAAP

Phone: 833-879-1365. Apply through BenefitsCal or in person at 1460 Mission Street.

County cash-assistance programs for eligible low-income adults without dependent children. Ask about shelter connected to the application process; neither cash aid nor shelter is automatic.

Official CAAP page

CalWORKs for families with children

Phone: 855-557-5100. Apply online or visit 170 Otis Street or 3120 Mission Street.

Tell the worker immediately if the family urgently needs housing, food, clothing, rent, utilities or medical help; emergency help depends on eligibility.

Official 2026 application steps

Medi-Cal health coverage

Phone: 855-355-5757. Apply through BenefitsCal, by phone or at an SFHSA center. Low-income California residents may qualify regardless of age or immigration status, but eligibility review is required.

Official Medi-Cal information

SFHSA service centers and mobile service

In-person benefits help is available at 1460 Mission Street, 1440 Harrison Street, 3120 Mission Street, 170 Otis Street and 2 Gough Street for older adults and people with disabilities. Confirm hours before traveling.

Important: 1235 Mission Street is permanently closed. The Mobile Benefits Center calendar offers additional temporary locations and can issue EBT cards when appropriate.

  • Official relocation notice
  • Mobile Benefits Center calendar

Free Muni Access Pass

A 12-month Muni-only pass for eligible people experiencing homelessness in San Francisco. Applicants must have contacted a Coordinated Entry Access Point within the previous six months. It does not cover BART.

Apply online, by mail, or at the SFMTA Customer Service Center, 11 South Van Ness Avenue, weekdays 8 a.m.–5 p.m.

Applying without a home address

Use an authorized mail program rather than an address that has not agreed to receive mail. Project Homeless Connect offers mail service, and Bayview Drop-In lists weekday mail service. The St. Anthony Tech Lab provides free computers, printing, scanning, fax and charging.

Never pay someone who promises a job or asks for money for an application.

8. Identification, legal help and immigration

Project Homeless Connect — identification help

Address: 1031 Franklin Street, second floor, San Francisco, CA 94109

Resource line: 855-588-7968. Eligible participants may receive a no-fee California ID voucher and help seeking birth certificates or passports. Help depends on eligibility, appointments and funds.

Official identification and mail services

California homeless birth-certificate fee waiver

A person experiencing homelessness may request a certified California birth record without the normal fee. A qualifying homeless-services provider must complete the affidavit; identity and record requirements still apply.

Free legal and social-service help for people experiencing or at imminent risk of homelessness, including disability benefits, eviction, SSI and immigration documentation. New-client drop-in is Tuesday–Friday, 1–3 p.m.; closed Mondays.

9. Families, young people, survivors, LGBTQ+ people and veterans

Domestic violence — immediate safety

Call 911 for immediate danger. For confidential support, call La Casa de las Madres at 877-503-1850 or WOMAN, Inc. at 877-384-3578, both 24 hours.

Confidential shelter addresses are not published. If an abusive person may monitor the device, use a safe telephone if possible.

Official survivor resources

Riley Center and Brennan House

24-hour support line: 415-255-0165. St. Vincent de Paul provides confidential survivor support, emergency shelter, transitional housing, case management and advocacy.

Brennan House accepts all genders, including male survivors, and children. Availability requires assessment.

Official domestic-violence services

SafeHouse Hope Center

Address: 1089 Mission Street, San Francisco, CA 94103

Drop-in help for women experiencing homelessness and sexual exploitation: food, bathrooms, hygiene, computers and connections to housing, legal and health services. It is not a guaranteed overnight bed.

10. Help for people with pets

Tell every shelter or access point about an animal before accepting placement. A pet, service animal and emotional-support animal may be treated differently. Do not surrender an animal before asking about a kennel, temporary care, food or veterinary support.

Veterinary Street Outreach Services — Vet SOS

Phone: 415-355-2248. Free twice-monthly pop-up veterinary clinics for unhoused San Francisco pet owners and some recently housed clients.

Wellness exams, vaccines, testing, supplies and referrals for free spay or neuter care. Appointment and eligibility confirmation are required; this is not emergency care.

Official eligibility and appointment instructions

Full Belly Bus

Phone: 415-635-1007. Free pet food, veterinary wellness help, grooming and supplies to unhoused people with dogs and cats through mobile events.

The Food 4 Paws page lists recurring distributions. Dates change; call or check before traveling.

Current pet-food locations and resources

SF SPCA Community Veterinary Clinic

Address: 4527 Mission Street, San Francisco, CA 94112

Text: 415-554-3084. Hours: Wednesday–Saturday, 8 a.m.–6 p.m.

Affordable preventive care and treatment for selected minor conditions. It does not provide emergencies, X-rays, laboratory diagnostics, adoption or health certificates.

Official clinic services and booking

Pet-aware shelter options

Lark-Inn publishes a pet kennel for youth ages 18–24. Other shelter rules differ. When calling 628-652-8000 or an access point, state the animal type, size and whether it is a trained service animal.

A kennel or animal-friendly policy does not guarantee a human shelter bed or space for the animal.
