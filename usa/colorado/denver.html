Denver homeless and housing-crisis help

Immediate danger, police, fire, or ambulance: 911
Denver services and shelter guidance: 311 or 720-913-1311
Food, benefits, and local referrals: 211 or 866-760-6489
Mental-health or substance-use crisis: call or text 988
Domestic-violence crisis: 303-318-9989, 24 hours

Last verified: November 12, 2026

Beds, meals, funding, routes, hours, and eligibility can change. Call or check the linked provider page before traveling when possible. No listing guarantees a bed, meal, treatment, payment, benefit, or job. Call 911 for immediate danger or a medical emergency.

Denver uses different shelter access points for men, women, families, and young people. Start with the access point that matches your household. Tell staff your location, age, household members, health or mobility needs, pregnancy, service animal or pet, and whether you need transportation. Ask what to do if no bed is available.

Best first step: visit a city walk-up access point below, or call 311 for current shelter and outreach guidance. Families with children should call the Salvation Army Connection Center. Young people ages 12–24 should contact Urban Peak. Placement depends on current capacity and eligibility.

1. Urgent help, outreach, and severe weather

Emergency services — 911

Phone: 911.

Call for immediate violence, fire, overdose, unconsciousness, severe breathing trouble, heavy bleeding, serious injury, or another life-threatening emergency. Give the exact location or nearest landmark and describe the danger first.

City of Denver — shelter and emergency guidance

Denver 311 and outreach

Phone: 311 inside Denver or 720-913-1311.

Use it for current city-service information, shelter guidance, an outreach request, or connection to the Clinical Homeless Outreach Team for behavioral-health support. Give the person's exact location and immediate needs. Outreach cannot guarantee shelter.

City of Denver — Clinical Homeless Outreach Team

Street Engagement Team

Request help: call 311 or 720-913-2000.

Unarmed civilian outreach, wellness checks, de-escalation, and connections to shelter, health, and social services. Call 911 instead when there is an immediate threat to life or safety.

City of Denver — Street Engagement Team

Severe-weather shelter updates

Text: send INDOORS to 67283.

When dangerous weather is expected, use the normal access points unless the city announces a different plan. Capacity and activation dates change, so check the city notice or call 311.

City of Denver — severe-weather shelter plans

988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline

Call or text: 988, 24 hours.

Support for suicidal thoughts, emotional crisis, or an urgent mental-health or substance-use concern. Call 911 when there is immediate physical danger.

2. Shelter and housing access

City shelter access — start here

Denver's walk-up access points assess needs and may connect people to an available shelter or transportation. Access is divided by household type. A visit or call does not guarantee a bed.

Free HOST circulator: the city publishes a free Monday–Friday bus, generally 8:30 a.m.–3:30 p.m., connecting major shelter, day-service, and health locations. Ask an access point for the current route.

City of Denver — find shelter

Lawrence Street Community Center — men

Address: 2222 Lawrence Street, Denver, CO 80205

Phone: 303-294-0157. Published hours: daily, 7:00 a.m.–8:00 p.m.

Walk-up shelter connection for men. The city advises arriving before 6:00 p.m. for transportation to shelter. At 2:30 p.m., access opens more broadly to people seeking shelter, dinner, or cold-weather help.

City of Denver — walk-up shelter details

Crossroads — men

Address: 1901 29th Street, Denver, CO 80216

Phone: 720-305-4640. Published intake: 10:00 a.m. daily, as space allows.

This is a walk-up option and does not provide transportation. Arrive early and keep another shelter plan because entry is not guaranteed.

Phone: 303-294-0241. Published access hours: daily, 4:30 p.m.–7:30 a.m.; the city advises arriving between 4:00 and 4:30 p.m.

Catholic Charities operates this shelter. The city's emergency listing does not state that religious participation is required.

14. Pets and service animals

Service animals at city shelters

The City of Denver states that service animals are allowed at shelters. Tell the access worker about the animal and any space or mobility needs. An emotional-support animal or pet may be treated differently from a trained service animal.

City of Denver — walk-up shelter information

Denver Animal Shelter — temporary pet housing

Program coordinator: 720-337-1813. If you need a caseworker: Denver Human Services at 720-944-3666.

For qualifying Denver residents facing domestic violence, hospitalization, treatment, or rehabilitation, temporary care is usually limited to about two weeks. Severe-cold placements may operate below the city's temperature threshold. A caseworker referral, available capacity, and safe animal behavior are required.

Denver Animal Shelter — temporary pet housing

Pet food, veterinary care, and pet-friendly resources

The city publishes links for pet food, low-cost or free veterinary care, and limited pet-friendly shelter resources. Availability changes, so call each provider. Do not surrender or abandon an animal based only on an online listing.

15. A practical plan for today

  1. If there is immediate danger or a medical emergency, call 911. For a mental-health or substance-use crisis without immediate physical danger, call or text 988.
  2. Use the access point matching your household: Lawrence Street or Crossroads for men, Samaritan House for women, the Connection Center for families, Urban Peak for ages 12–24, or SafeHouse for domestic violence.
  3. Ask about transportation and a backup plan if there is no bed. Check severe-weather activation by texting INDOORS to 67283.
  4. For food and hygiene today, call 211 or the Food Resource Hotline, then check St. Francis Center, Metro Caring, Gathering Place, or a listed church meal.
  5. Use Denver Human Services for benefits, Stout Street Health for care, Denver Workforce Services for work, and Metro Caring or St. Francis Center for ID help.
  6. Write down the program, the person you spoke with, the time, the next step, and requested documents. Keep court and benefit deadlines.
