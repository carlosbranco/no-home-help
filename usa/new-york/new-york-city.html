1. Emergency shelters

If you have nowhere to sleep tonight: go to the intake center that matches your household. City intake centers are open 24 hours a day, every day, including holidays. Applying at intake is the first step; the intake sites below are not the final shelter placement. Immigration status does not affect access, and interpreters are available.

Single adults are strongly encouraged to bring identification, but NYC says it is not required. Families should bring identification and documents for all household members if possible. Call 311 for directions or reasonable-accommodation information. The City does not provide transportation to intake.

3. Churches and religious organizations

The two religious organizations below provide practical help to anyone. Their faith identity is included so visitors can make an informed choice.

The Bowery Mission

Tradition: Christian, operated by Christian Herald Association.

Practical help: free meals, showers, clothing, medical outreach, emergency shelter, case management, employment help, and pastoral care.

Participation: chapel precedes meals, but attendance is not required to receive a meal. Longer residential programs are faith-based; ask about program expectations before enrolling.

Contacts: Bowery Campus, 212-674-3456; Tribeca shelter, 212-226-6214.

Church of the Holy Apostles

Tradition: Episcopal.

6. Free or low-cost medical care

Call 911 for a medical emergency. For a mental health or substance use crisis, call or text 988.

NYC Care

Phone: 646-692-2273

Services: primary care, mental health and substance use treatment, prescriptions, vaccinations, and other care through NYC Health + Hospitals.

Cost and eligibility: sliding-scale fees can start at $0. The program is for New Yorkers who do not have health insurance and cannot afford private insurance, regardless of immigration status. Enrollment is not automatic.

8. Phone charging and internet

9. Free or reduced transportation

No general free-transit program for all adults experiencing homelessness was confirmed. These official programs may help:

  • Fair Fares NYC: eligible low-income residents ages 18 through 64 can receive 50% off subway, eligible bus, and Access-A-Ride fares. Immigration status does not affect eligibility. Call 311 or check eligibility and apply.
  • Students in temporary housing: eligible students can receive school transportation or an OMNY card; parents or guardians may receive a card to accompany a child. Ask the child's school or read the official school transportation rules.
  • DHS Travel Request: people applying for shelter or already in shelter who have a confirmed housing option outside NYC may qualify for one-way bus, train, or air travel. Ask shelter or intake staff. Official DHS information.

11. Employment assistance

Workforce1 Career Centers

Contact: call 311 and ask for “Workforce1.”

Services: free one-to-one career help, job matching, recruitment events, résumé and interview workshops, job-search resources, and training referrals. Help is available online, by phone, and in person at centers across the five boroughs.

Eligibility: people age 18 or older who are legally authorized to work in the United States. ACCESS NYC also lists NYC residence as a requirement; ask Workforce1 how to document residence if you do not have a permanent address.

Last verified: 2026-11-10

  • Official eligibility and application information
  • Official center addresses
  • Current Jobs NYC hiring events

New York Public Library one-to-one job support

Services: free appointments for help writing or formatting a résumé and cover letter, completing applications, finding job boards, and locating training or interview clothing.

Availability: in person or remotely by phone or Google Meet. Appointments open weekly and may fill.

Official appointment information

The Bowery Mission residential employment support

Phone: 212-226-6214

Services: job-search assistance, career coaching, résumé support, training, tutoring, and interview preparation for people accepted into its faith-based residential programs.

Important: this is not a general walk-in job center. Ask about residential-program admission and expectations.

Official program and admission information

Practical steps without a permanent address

  1. Ask a drop-in center, shelter, or case manager for help establishing a safe mailing contact. Never use an organization's address without permission.
  2. Use a phone number and email account that you can check reliably. Public libraries offer computers and internet for applications.
  3. Keep copies of your résumé, identification, work-authorization documents, applications, and employer responses when possible.
  4. Tell Workforce1 or library staff which documents, phone access, interview clothing, or transportation you lack so they can identify appropriate referrals.
  5. Check the official Jobs NYC events page shortly before attending; dates, registration rules, and locations change.

12. Free legal aid

NYC Office of Civil Justice

Contact: call 311. For eviction or housing-court help, say “Tenant Helpline.”

Services: free legal help may be available for eviction, housing, immigration, wage theft, employment, and other civil matters. You do not need to be an HRA client, and immigration status does not prevent access to the service.

Important: eligibility and representation depend on the case. A lawyer working through the City's program will not ask you to pay.

13. Documents and identification

IDNYC

Service: free city photo identification for NYC residents age 10 and older.

If you have no stable address: a City agency, nonprofit, religious institution, hospital, or health clinic can provide an official letter establishing NYC residency. The card can omit an address. An eligible organization may also permit its address to appear as a “care-of” address.

How to start: review the document guide and make an enrollment-center appointment. Approval depends on the documents accepted by IDNYC.

  • Official IDNYC overview
  • Documents and forms for applicants without a home address

For a birth certificate, Social Security card, state ID, or driver's license, ask a shelter or drop-in case manager about the correct issuing agency, accepted address proof, and possible fee assistance. Do not pay an unofficial service to obtain government documents.

14. Mailing address

USPS General Delivery

Service: free mail pickup at an authorized Post Office without a street address. Availability, identification rules, and how long mail is held depend on the facility.

Before using it: contact the Post Office to confirm that it offers General Delivery and ask for the correct ZIP Code and pickup rules.

Address format:
YOUR NAME
GENERAL DELIVERY
CITY STATE ZIP

Official USPS instructions

Some shelters, drop-in centers, nonprofits, and religious organizations may receive mail for active clients. Ask the organization first; never use its address without permission.

17. Domestic violence support

NYC 24-Hour Hope Hotline

Phone: 800-621-HOPE (4673)

Emergency: call or text 911 if you are in immediate danger.

Services: free, confidential support for domestic or intimate-partner violence, sexual violence, trafficking, stalking, elder abuse, and other crimes. Advocates can help with safety planning, shelter options, and referrals in multiple languages.

Privacy: shelter addresses are confidential and are not published here. If an abuser may monitor your device, calling can be safer than browsing.

18. Help for people with pets

Important: NYC DHS does not allow pets to live in shelter with clients. Service animals are permitted. Requests involving emotional support animals are considered individually. Do not surrender an animal before asking about prevention or temporary-care options.

Animal Care Centers of NYC and pet-support options

Phone: 212-788-4000

Help: ACC counselors may be able to connect owners with resources intended to keep people and pets together, reduced-cost veterinary care, or placement options. Appointments are recommended for intake.

Domestic violence: call 800-621-HOPE (4673) and tell the advocate that you need safety with a pet; some programs may allow survivors and pets to stay together.

19. Airport transportation

Public-transit fares and service can change. Check the official MTA pages before traveling. For the correct shelter intake center after reaching the city, call 311.

John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK)

Lower-cost route toward Manhattan: take the AirTrain to Jamaica Station, then take the subway toward Manhattan. The MTA also lists an AirTrain connection at Howard Beach.

Approximate fare: $11.75 for most riders: $8.75 AirTrain plus $3 subway. Unlimited passes do not cover AirTrain.

Time: varies by terminal, wait time, destination, and overnight service; the MTA advises allowing extra time.

Official step-by-step MTA guide

LaGuardia Airport (LGA)

Lower-cost route toward Midtown: take the free Q70 bus to Jackson Heights-Roosevelt Avenue or 74 Street-Broadway, then take the subway toward Manhattan. The M60 bus connects the airport with Upper Manhattan.

Approximate fare: $3 for the subway plus free Q70 bus; the M60 costs $3 for most riders.

Time: the MTA says the trip can take up to an hour or more depending on the route and traffic.

Official step-by-step MTA guide

20. Other useful resources

NYC homeless outreach and 24-hour drop-in centers

Call 311 to request outreach for a person living on the street or subway. Call 911 for a medical emergency, immediate danger, or a person on subway tracks.

Drop-in centers offer a place to come indoors, hot meals, clothing, showers, laundry, case management, health connections, job help, and benefits help. All listed City centers are open 24 hours a day, every day. They are not shelters with beds.

  • Official homeless outreach information
  • Official drop-in center list

Extreme cold or heat: call 311 or ask a drop-in center about current warming or cooling locations. During a City Code Blue, additional open-door rules apply at shelters, drop-in centers, and designated hospitals.
