Toronto — help for people experiencing homelessness

Life-threatening emergency: 911
Shelter or 24-hour respite referral: Central Intake, 416-338-4766 or 1-877-338-3398, 24 hours
Someone outside who needs outreach: 311
Mental-health crisis: 211 for the Toronto Community Crisis Service; call or text 988 for suicide crisis
Violence against a woman: 416-863-0511; immediate danger, 911

Last verified: November 11, 2026

Hours, eligibility and available spaces can change. Call before travelling when possible. No listing guarantees a bed, meal, payment, appointment, document or job. During severe weather, check the City page or call 311 for activated sites.

When asking for help, say where you are, your age, any urgent health or accessibility needs, whether children or a pet are with you, and whether you have a safe place tonight. Central Intake is the main route into the City shelter system; most shelter addresses are not direct walk-in access.

1. Emergency and first contact

911 — emergencies

Phone: 911. Call for immediate danger, a serious medical emergency, fire or a crime in progress.

Give the exact location or nearest intersection, what is happening and how many people need help. If it is safe, stay on the line and follow the operator's instructions.

Last verified: November 11, 2026

Make the right call — City of Toronto

Central Intake — shelter and respite access

Toronto: 416-338-4766. Toll-free: 1-877-338-3398. Available 24 hours, every day; you can also call 311.

Central Intake matches people with available emergency shelter or 24-hour respite spaces. Say if you are a family, youth, woman, Indigenous person, couple, have accessibility needs or have a pet. A call does not guarantee an immediate space; ask what to do if nothing is available and keep the callback number active if you can.

Last verified: November 11, 2026

Central Intake — City of Toronto

311 Toronto

Inside Toronto: 311. Outside the city: 416-392-2489. TTY: 711. Available 24 hours.

Ask for street outreach, an activated warming centre, City services or help reaching Central Intake. For a person outside, give a precise location, description and any immediate risks. 311 is not emergency dispatch.

Last verified: November 11, 2026

311 Toronto — City of Toronto

211 Ontario — community and social services

Call: 211, 24 hours, in many languages. The service is free and confidential.

Ask for nearby food, clothing, housing help, health services, financial assistance or a crisis team. The navigator can explain eligibility and access, but cannot promise a place or service.

Last verified: November 11, 2026

211 Ontario

Streets to Homes — outreach

Request outreach: call 311. Outreach teams engage people sleeping outside and can work on shelter, identification, income and housing options.

Give the nearest address or intersection and describe the person's location. Outreach is voluntary and response is based on urgency and team availability; call 911 for an immediate threat to life.

Last verified: November 11, 2026

Outreach or City information: 311. Questions about belongings stored after an encampment move: 416-338-1876.

City outreach staff offer shelter and housing connections before and during encampment responses. Ask staff for written information about storage, retrieval deadlines and where items are taken. For immediate danger, call 911.

Last verified: November 11, 2026

2. Shelter, respite and street outreach

City shelter system

Toronto shelters operate 24 hours and may provide meals, laundry, case management, health or harm-reduction support. Sites serve different groups and some addresses are confidential.

Access: call Central Intake at 416-338-4766 or 1-877-338-3398. Do not travel to a listed shelter expecting admission unless Central Intake or the shelter tells you to go.

Last verified: November 11, 2026

Shelters — City of Toronto

24-hour respite sites

Respite sites provide an indoor place to rest, meals and referrals. Toronto states that its 24-hour respite sites are pet-friendly; capacity and rules still vary.

Access: Central Intake, 416-338-4766. Seasonal winter respite sites may accept walk-ins from November 15 to April 15; confirm the current site and access route before travelling.

Last verified: November 11, 2026

24-hour respite sites — City of Toronto

Warming centres

Warming centres are temporary, walk-in sites activated during cold weather. They offer a warm indoor space, washrooms and meals, and are pet-friendly. Central Intake referral is not required.

Before travelling: call 311 or check the City page to confirm that centres are activated and which addresses are open. Activation can change with the forecast.

Last verified: November 11, 2026

5. Health, mental health and substance use

Health811 — nurse advice

Phone: 811, free and confidential, 24 hours. A registered nurse can help decide whether to use emergency care, a clinic or self-care.

Health811 is not an ambulance and cannot diagnose every problem. Call 911 for chest pain, severe breathing trouble, unconsciousness, major bleeding, overdose or another immediate threat to life.

Last verified: November 11, 2026

Health811 Ontario

Toronto Community Crisis Service

Access: call 211, 24 hours. Free, city-wide, non-police mobile crisis support for people aged 16 and older.

Use it for a mental-health crisis when no one is in immediate danger. The 211 navigator assesses the call and may dispatch a community crisis team; response depends on safety and availability.

Last verified: November 11, 2026

Toronto Community Crisis Service — City of Toronto

988 — suicide crisis

Call or text: 988, 24 hours. Use it if you are thinking about suicide or worried about someone else.

For an attempt in progress, immediate physical danger or a medical emergency, call 911. You can contact 988 even if you are not sure how serious the situation is.

Last verified: November 11, 2026

A bed or in-person response is not guaranteed. Call 911 for immediate danger or a life-threatening medical emergency.

6. Income, housing and transit support

Ontario Works and Emergency Assistance

Apply by phone: 1-888-999-1142, Monday to Friday during business hours; online applications are available at any time.

Ontario Works may provide income and employment support if eligible. Emergency Assistance may apply in an urgent crisis such as having no shelter, food or essential medication. If you have no fixed address, Toronto says you may use the nearest Employment and Social Services office address. Approval is not automatic.

Last verified: November 11, 2026

Ontario Works — City of Toronto

Ontario Disability Support Program

Application support: 1-888-999-1142. ODSP provides income and employment support to eligible people with disabilities.

The application requires medical and financial information and can take time. Ask Ontario Works or a community legal clinic about interim help if you have no income while waiting.

Last verified: November 11, 2026

ODSP — Government of Ontario

Toronto Employment and Social Services offices

Use the City list to find the office serving your postal code or nearest location. Offices can help with Ontario Works, documents, employment services and referrals.

Check whether an appointment is required and what to bring. If you have no address, explain this when contacting the office.

Last verified: November 11, 2026

Social Insurance Number

Service Canada: 1-866-274-6627. Apply online, by mail or in person for a SIN if eligible; Canada no longer issues plastic SIN cards.

Use only the official Government of Canada service. Do not carry a written SIN unless necessary and do not give it to a landlord or food bank.

Free volunteer tax clinics assist eligible people with simple returns. Check the clinic's appointment, income and document rules.

Veterans Affairs Canada: 1-866-522-2122. Staff can connect an eligible veteran or family member with case management, benefits and community services.

Good Shepherd's drop-in also lists veterans navigation. Say that you served when contacting Central Intake or a drop-in so staff can identify specialist support.

Last verified: November 11, 2026

Veteran homelessness support — Veterans Affairs Canada

Veterans Emergency Fund

Eligible veterans and certain family members facing a financial emergency may apply for help with essentials such as food, clothing, uninsured health costs or shelter.

Apply or ask for help: 1-866-522-2122. The program verifies service, identity, residence and need; payment is assessed case by case and is not guaranteed.

Last verified: November 11, 2026

If you have a pet

Tell Central Intake about the animal before accepting a referral. Toronto states that 24-hour respite sites and activated warming centres are pet-friendly, but not every shelter can accommodate every animal.

Ask what supplies or documentation are needed and whether the pet can stay with you. Do not abandon or hide an animal to enter a site.

Last verified: November 11, 2026

Pet services in homeless shelters — City of Toronto

HelpingPawsTO

For clients of participating shelters and 24-hour homelessness services, HelpingPawsTO may provide free veterinary care such as an exam, vaccination, spay or neuter, microchip and parasite treatment.

Access is by staff referral, not direct walk-in. Supplies and appointments depend on availability. Ask your shelter or service worker.
