Vancouver — help for people experiencing homelessness

Life-threatening emergency: 911
Current shelter spaces: call or text 211, or check the live BC211 shelter list
Street outreach: 604-665-3318; outreach cell 604-968-1825
Health advice: 811, 24 hours
Violence or exploitation: call or text VictimLinkBC at 1-800-563-0808, 24 hours

Last verified: November 12, 2026

Hours, eligibility and spaces can change. Call before travelling when possible. A listing does not guarantee a bed, meal, payment, appointment, document or job. BC211's Vancouver shelter list is the best starting point for current bed and mat information.

When asking for help, say where you are, your age, whether you have a safe place tonight, and whether children, a partner, a pet, large belongings, accessibility needs or urgent health needs are involved. Ask the worker to explain the intake process before you travel.

1. Emergency and first contact

911 — immediate danger

Phone: 911. Call for a serious medical emergency, suspected overdose, fire, violence or a crime in progress.

Give the exact address, nearest intersection or visible landmark. Say what happened and whether the person is conscious and breathing. If it is safe, stay on the line and follow the operator's instructions.

Last verified: November 12, 2026

Emergency guidance — City of Vancouver

BC211 — shelter, food and local services

Call or text: 211. Current navigator hours are 9am to 9pm on weekdays, excluding statutory holidays. The website can be searched at any time.

Ask for a shelter that fits your age, gender, household, accessibility and pet needs. BC211 can also search for food, health, legal, financial, newcomer and transport services. It cannot reserve or guarantee a bed.

City outreach workers can help with shelter, housing applications, income benefits, identification, health referrals, clothing, detox and harm-reduction supplies. The team also conducts street outreach.

VictimLinkBC — violence, crime and human trafficking

Call or text: 1-800-563-0808, 24 hours. The service is confidential, toll-free and multilingual.

VictimLinkBC can provide immediate support and referrals to transition houses, victim services, counselling and legal or safety-planning resources. You do not need to be certain that a crime occurred before contacting the service. Call 911 for immediate danger.

5. Health, mental health and substance use

HealthLink BC 811

Phone: 811, 24 hours. A navigator can find local care and connect callers to a registered nurse for non-emergency symptoms. Translation is available in more than 130 languages.

Use 811 to ask about walk-in care, community clinics, mental-health services or medication questions. Call 911 for severe breathing trouble, chest pain, unconsciousness, major bleeding, overdose or another immediate threat to life.

Last verified: November 12, 2026

HealthLink BC 811 services

Community health care without a regular doctor

Ask 811 or a shelter or outreach worker to find the appropriate Vancouver Coastal Health clinic. Explain if you have no fixed address, no phone, no valid health card, mobility needs or medication that may be lost or stolen.

Community health centres serve different areas and programs. Call first because many services require an appointment or have specific eligibility.

Last verified: November 12, 2026

6. Income, housing and transport support

Income and disability assistance

Phone: 1-866-866-0800. Apply through My Self Serve, by phone or at an income-assistance office. Tell the worker if you urgently need food, shelter or medical attention.

Vancouver offices include Dockside at 180 Main Street, Kiwassa at 205 Powell Street, Strathcona at 687 Powell Street and other neighbourhood locations. Offices normally close from noon to 1pm; confirm before going.

Last verified: November 12, 2026

Apply for assistance | Office locations

If you do not have all required identification

Do not assume you cannot apply. Provincial policy allows temporary assistance in some cases while required ID is being pursued after identity has been verified.

Ask the intake worker what can be used in your case and request help from City outreach or a shelter worker to replace missing documents. Eligibility is assessed individually.

Last verified: November 12, 2026

BC Housing Registry — subsidized housing

Lower Mainland: 604-433-2218. Toll-free: 1-800-257-7756. Eligible low-income people, including single people or couples who are homeless or at risk, can apply.

The Housing Listings are not a vacancy list, and some buildings require a separate application directly to the provider. Apply to every suitable building and keep contact information updated.

City outreach at 392 Powell Street can help with housing applications and move-in steps. Shelter and community workers may also complete referrals or supporting forms when a program requires professional verification.

No worker can guarantee a unit or move-in date. Keep copies of applications, record building codes and report changes to your phone, mailing address or household.

Vancouver Welcome Centre — ISSofBC

Protect yourself from job scams

Do not pay for a job offer, send money to an employer, deposit a cheque and return part of it, or share a SIN before a legitimate employer needs it for payroll.

Ask WorkBC to review a suspicious offer. Keep your SIN confidential and do not use it as general identification.

8. Identification, legal help and tenancy

Social Insurance Number

A SIN is needed to work in Canada and access some government programs. There is no fee to apply. Service Canada no longer issues plastic SIN cards.

SIN Program: 1-866-274-6627. Required documents depend on citizenship or immigration status. Ask a shelter or outreach worker for help scanning documents or arranging a safe mailing address.

Last verified: November 12, 2026

Apply for a SIN — Government of Canada

BC identification card

A BCID is government photo identification for people who do not drive. An appointment, accepted identity documents and a fee are normally required at a driver licensing office.

Before paying or travelling, use the ICBC document checker. City outreach can help apply for or safely store identification and may identify help with replacement costs.

Last verified: November 12, 2026

Apply for a BCID — ICBC

Benefits and tax mail without a permanent home

The Canada Revenue Agency says a person without a permanent home can use a temporary address, such as a shelter, to continue receiving payments and mail.

Ask the shelter or drop-in for permission before using its address. Check how often mail must be collected and what name or identification is required. Update each government department separately when the address changes.

Last verified: November 12, 2026

CRA help for people experiencing housing insecurity

Legal Aid BC

Greater Vancouver: 604-408-2172. Elsewhere in BC: 1-866-577-2525. Legal Aid assesses eligibility for covered legal matters.

Have court papers, deadlines and available proof of income ready. If the matter is urgent, say the next hearing, eviction or response date at the start of the call.

Last verified: November 12, 2026

Call Legal Aid BC

FIRST UNITED legal advocacy

Phone: 604-251-3323. Advocates help with income assistance, disability benefits, Employment Insurance, employment standards, government debt, tenancy and related issues.

Drop-in intake times and the service location can change. Check the current page before going and bring notices, decisions, identification and deadlines if available.

Last verified: November 12, 2026

Legal advocacy — FIRST UNITED

Tenancy and eviction help

Contact a legal or tenant organization immediately after receiving an eviction notice. Do not ignore the notice; dispute deadlines can be short. FIRST UNITED and other organizations listed by the City can explain options.

The Vancouver Rent Bank may help with an eligible temporary financial crisis, but an application does not by itself stop an eviction. Keep copies of every notice, payment and message.

Last verified: November 12, 2026

Renter supports and protections — City of Vancouver

Lost or stolen documents

Make a list of what is missing and contact each issuer. Ask City outreach, Directions, DEWC or another trusted worker to help with applications, fees, photocopies, secure storage and a mailing address.

Do not send original documents unless an official process specifically requires them. Record application numbers and keep digital copies in a secure account when possible.

11. Cold, heat and smoke

Winter shelters and Extreme Weather Response

Temporary winter shelters normally add spaces during the colder months. Extreme Weather Response sites open only when an alert is active. Criteria include cold, snow, freezing rain, severe wind and heavy rain.

Use the live BC211 list immediately before travelling. An address published for the winter does not mean the site is active or has space tonight.

Last verified: November 12, 2026

Shelters and winter response — City of Vancouver

Warming centres

The City activates warming centres when the temperature reaches or feels like -4°C or below. These centres provide a warm indoor space but are not set up with beds or mats.

Activation locations are announced with extreme-weather alerts. Check the City shelter page, the BC211 list or ask a shelter or outreach worker before going.

Last verified: November 12, 2026

Current winter response — City of Vancouver

Cooling centres

During hot weather, City community centres and libraries provide air-conditioned spaces. No registration, payment or identification is required; seating, drinking water and washrooms are available.

Most community-centre cooling sites accept well-behaved pets, but Evelyne Saller and Carnegie do not, and pets are not allowed in library cooling centres. Check the current map and hours during a heat event.

Last verified: November 12, 2026

Hot-weather resources — City of Vancouver

Wildfire smoke and cleaner air

City facilities with cleaner air operate during their regular hours and may provide relief from smoke. Limit strenuous outdoor activity, drink water and carry prescribed rescue medication.

Call 811 for health advice. Call 911 for severe breathing trouble, chest pain, fainting or another immediate emergency.

Last verified: November 12, 2026

Cleaner-air guidance — City of Vancouver
